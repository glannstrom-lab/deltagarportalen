TITLE: Skills inventory and skills mapping – how to do it
SUMMARY: How to make a skills inventory step by step, with a skills matrix template for Excel and the difference from a skills analysis.

A skills inventory (kompetensinventering), sometimes called skills mapping (kompetenskartläggning), is a review where you write down everything you can do: from jobs, education, hobbies, club activities and daily life. The result is a list you can use for your CV, for interviews and for choosing your next step. If you want to start with the question "what skills do I have", there is a separate guide: [What skills do I have?](/knowledge-base/article/vilka-kompetenser-har-jag).

## Why make a skills inventory?

- **In your job search:** you find skills from other areas that you can use in the new one, and things you forgot to mention.
- **In your career planning:** you see where you are today and what is missing for a goal. Then the next step is a skills analysis or [gap analysis](/knowledge-base/article/kompetensutveckling-plan).
- **For your self-confidence:** it is easy to think that what you can do is obvious. On paper you can see that it is not. If you recognize this, [Imposter syndrome](/knowledge-base/article/imposter-syndrome) may be worth reading.

## The difference between inventory, mapping and analysis

People use these words in a mixed way, and in daily life they mean almost the same thing:

| Word | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Kompetensinventering (skills inventory) | You list what you can do |
| Kompetenskartläggning (skills mapping) | The same thing, often with a level and examples, and sometimes done by an employer or a case officer for several people |
| Kompetensanalys (skills analysis) | You compare what you can do with what a job, a role or a goal requires |

This page covers the first step and how to take it further to the analysis.

## Different kinds of skills

**Hard skills** are things you can point to: tools and systems, machines, languages, certificates, authorizations, administrative tasks.

**Soft skills** are about how you work: communication, cooperation, leadership, planning and setting priorities.

**Transferable skills** work in many jobs, for example problem solving, organizing, customer contact and teaching others. They are often what moves with you if you change industry.

## How to make a skills inventory

### Step 1: Collect material

Go through old CVs, grades and certificates, employer certificates (arbetsgivarintyg), feedback you have received, your LinkedIn profile and things you have done outside work: hobbies, clubs, volunteer work, taking care of home and family.

### Step 2: Sort

Make a list with headings. A simple division:

- Technical skills and tools
- Industry knowledge
- Soft skills, with one example of when you used them
- Languages
- Certificates and authorizations
- Other, for example from a hobby or club activities

### Step 3: Set a level

Use a scale that you understand yourself, for example beginner, can work independently, advanced and expert. It is good to write one sentence about how you know: "I have done it myself in several projects" says more than a level alone.

### Step 4: Look for hidden skills

Ask yourself what you actually do in the things you already do:

- Do you keep the accounts for a club? Then you know economy and you are careful.
- Have you organized a family reunion or an event? Then you can plan, coordinate and negotiate.
- Have you learned something completely new as an adult, for example a language or a program? Then you have shown that you can learn.
- Have you helped someone through difficult times? Then you have practiced listening and holding on.

This is not about making yourself seem special. It is only about putting words on what is true.

### Step 5: Ask someone else

Ask a colleague, a former manager, a friend or your arbetskonsulent (work consultant): "What do you usually ask me for help with?" Other people often see what you take for granted. You can find more ways in [Discover your strengths](/knowledge-base/article/upptack-dina-styrkor).

## Skills matrix – a template to make in Excel

A skills matrix is a table that gives an overview. You can make it in Excel, Google Sheets or on paper. The columns can be:

| Skill | Level | Do I use it now? | Do I want to develop it? | Is it needed for my goal? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Excel | Advanced | Yes | No | Yes |
| Project management | Medium | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Python | Beginner | No | Yes | Maybe |

Read the matrix like this: what is strong and needed for the goal is what you highlight. What you want to develop and what is needed is your list to work toward. What is neither needed nor interesting, you can leave.

## Tools for skills mapping and skills analysis

You do not need any special program. A document or a spreadsheet template goes a long way. The portal has two tools that help you further: [Skills gap compared with a dream job](/skills-gap-analysis) for the analysis, and the CV builder to collect your skills in one place. ## Skills mapping in IT and other jobs with many tools

The method is the same, but the division is more detailed. In IT you can divide into languages, frameworks, systems, operating environments, methods and the soft parts, such as explaining technical things to others. It is better to write what you have done with the tool than only its name. The same way of thinking works in construction, health care and other jobs where equipment and authorizations matter.

## Use the result

- **CV:** highlight what matches the job ad, not everything. See [CV basics](/knowledge-base/article/cv-grunder).
- **LinkedIn:** add the most important skills and ask contacts to confirm them.
- **Interview:** have a concrete example for each key skill. The [STAR method](/knowledge-base/article/kompetensbaserad-intervju-star-metoden) helps you to tell it.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a skills mapping?

A review where you, or an employer or case officer together with you, describe what knowledge, skills and experience you have. It can be the basis for a CV, a work placement, education or a development plan.

### What is the difference between a skills inventory and a skills analysis?

The inventory answers what you can do. The analysis compares that with what a role or a goal requires and shows what is missing.

### I think I cannot do anything special. What do I do?

Start by writing down what you do in a normal week, at home and in your free time. Then ask two people what they think you are good at. What feels easy for you is often what someone else would pay for.

### My skills feel out of date.

Communication, planning and problem solving do not get old. If a tool or a technique has changed, you can see what is different and fill exactly that gap. See [Skills development – how to keep your skills relevant](/knowledge-base/article/kompetensutveckling-guide).

## What do I do now

Take some time and write a list, without judging yourself. Sort it tomorrow. Then ask someone to read it and say what is missing. When you have it, you can take the next step: compare it with a job ad for the job you want.
