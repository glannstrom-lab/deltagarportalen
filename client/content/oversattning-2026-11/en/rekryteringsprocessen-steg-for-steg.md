TITLE: The hiring process step by step: from application to answer
SUMMARY: Screening, selection, interviews, tests, references and offer: what decides at each step and what you can do when you hear nothing.

A hiring process can go from application through selection and interview to references and an offer. The number of steps and their order are decided by the employer, so this guide shows seven steps that are often included, what decides at each one and what you can do yourself. In the time between the steps you often get no news, and that is one of the hardest parts.

## Step 1: Application and screening

You send in your CV and application, sometimes through an application form in a recruitment system. Some employers use questions in the form to sort out applicants who do not meet basic requirements, such as a driving licence, languages or permits. Others read all applications. How it works depends on the employer and how the system is set up. More about this is in [ATS systems: how they work and how to adapt your CV](/knowledge-base/article/ats-system-guide).

If you are stopped here, it is more often about requirements in the form or a CV that is hard to read than about you as a person. Write with the same words as the ad when it fits your experience.

## Step 2: Selection

Applications are read by a person: a hiring manager, HR or a consultant. Here the whole picture is judged: does the experience match, is the letter well thought out, is there something that stands out. Concrete facts about what you have done make it easier to choose you.

## Step 3: First contact

Often a short phone call or an email with a few questions. The aim can be to check basics: are you still interested, when can you start, what do you think about pay. Answer honestly and briefly.

## Step 4: Interviews

The number of interviews varies. The first interview is often about basic match, motivation and how you communicate. A second one often goes deeper, sometimes with a future colleague or a manager's manager. Read more in [The second interview](/knowledge-base/article/andra-intervjun) and [Prepare for the interview](/knowledge-base/article/intervju-forberedelser).

## Step 5: Test or work sample

Not all processes have this step. It can be an ability test, a personality test or a practical task. What they measure and how you prepare is in [Work psychology tests](/knowledge-base/article/arbetspsykologiska-tester). The line between a work sample and unpaid work is described in [Trial work without pay](/knowledge-base/article/provjobba-utan-lon).

## Step 6: References and possible checks

The employer can contact the references you gave. This is often done towards the end of the process, but the employer decides when. Read [References: how to choose and prepare them](/knowledge-base/article/referenser-guide).

Some employers also check information you gave, for example education. How long it takes depends on what is checked and who does it, so ask the recruiter. You have the right to know what information is collected and why. [Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten](https://www.imy.se) (the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection) explains your rights.

## Step 7: The offer

Last comes the offer: role, terms, start date. It is reasonable to ask questions and ask for time to think. Ask to get the offer in writing before you say yes. How to read the terms is in [How to read an employment contract](/knowledge-base/article/las-anstallningsavtal) and about the trial period in [How probationary employment works](/knowledge-base/article/provanstallning-guide).

## The silence between steps

This is the most common source of worry. Between the steps there are things you cannot see: someone is on leave, another candidate must finish their interview, a decision is waiting for approval. Silence therefore means neither yes nor no, it means that you do not know.

If you do not know where you stand, it is reasonable to ask. Do it last in every conversation: "What is the next step, and about when can I hear something?" And get in touch if it has taken longer than the recruiter said. How to word it is in [Following up your application](/knowledge-base/article/folja-upp-din-ansokan).

## What do I do now

Write down where you are in the process for each application: sent, selection, interview, test, references. You can keep track of them in the application tool in Jobin. Set a date for when you will get in touch if you have not received news. And use the waiting time for the next application, see [Effective application strategy](/knowledge-base/article/ansokningsstrategi).

## Common questions

### How does a hiring process work?

Application, selection, contact, interview, sometimes tests, references and offer. Which steps are included and in which order is decided by the employer.

### How long does a hiring process take?

It varies between employers and jobs. Ask in the first contact or after the interview when you can expect an answer.

### How do I know which step I am at if nobody has said?

Ask the recruiter: "What is the next step, and about when can I hear something?" It is a natural question to end a conversation with.

### How long does a background check take?

It depends on what is checked and who does the check. Ask the employer, and ask for information about what data is collected.

### Does it mean anything if checking references takes time?

It may be because the referees are hard to reach. You cannot read anything certain into it.

### Can I skip a step if I have already done something similar?

Ask, but do not count on it. Every employer follows its own process.
