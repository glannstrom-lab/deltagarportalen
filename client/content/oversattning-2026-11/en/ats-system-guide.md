TITLE: ATS systems and your CV: how they work and how to adapt
SUMMARY: What an ATS system is, what it does with your CV, and how to make an ATS-friendly CV that is also easy for a person to read.

An ATS system (in Swedish "rekryteringssystem", a recruitment system) is software that employers use to receive, read and organize applications. Your CV ends up in such a system when you apply through a company's application form. How much the system sorts out depends on how the employer has set it up. Here we go through what that means for your CV.

Are you looking for an ATS system to use as an employer? This page is written for people who are looking for a job.

## What is ATS?

ATS stands for Applicant Tracking System. The system collects applications in one place, saves each candidate's history and lets the recruiter search, filter and sort. Teamtailor, which is a Swedish recruitment system, [describes an ATS itself](https://www.teamtailor.com/sv/ordlista/rekryteringssystem/) as the place where a company gathers its whole recruitment, from application to decision. Other systems you may see are Workday, Greenhouse and Lever.

It is not always the same as a robot that reads and judges you. The system is a tool that a person uses, but what the tool does can differ a lot between employers.

## How does ATS work for you as a job seeker?

### Step 1: The system reads your CV

When you upload a file, the system tries to pick out information such as name, contact details, titles, years and skills and put them in fields. If the CV is hard to read, information can end up in the wrong place or be missing.

### Step 2: The recruiter filters and searches

The recruiter can search among the applications for words and phrases, and sort by how well they match the requirements. Some systems also have automatic scores or suggestions.

### Step 3: Screening questions

The automatic sorting often comes from the questions in the application form, not from the CV text. Teamtailor describes [screening questions](https://www.teamtailor.com/sv/ordlista/urvalsfragor/) (urvalsfrågor) as questions about objective requirements, for example availability, driving licence or languages, where a wrong answer can sort out the applicant directly if the employer has set it up that way. Read the questions carefully and answer truthfully. If it says that a driving licence is required and you do not have one, it does not help to tick yes.

## ATS-friendly CV: what usually matters

An ATS-friendly CV is a CV that is easy for both programs and people to read. Here is how to make it simple:

### Format

- Save as PDF or .docx, as the ad or the form asks.
- Try copying the text from the CV into Notepad (Anteckningar). If it comes out in the right order, it is easy for a program to read. If it looks messy, make the layout simpler.
- Avoid images with text, so that name and contact details are plain text.

### Layout

- One column is simplest. Columns, text boxes and tables can be read in the wrong order.
- Common fonts and clear headings.
- Contact details in the page itself, not in the header or footer.

### Headings

Use headings that everyone knows: profile, work experience, education, skills, languages. Creative headings such as "My journey" give the system and the reader something to interpret.

### Words from the ad

Read the ad and note the words it uses for tasks and requirements. If you have the experience, write it with the same words as the ad. If the ad says "kundservice" (customer service) and your CV says "kundorienterad" (customer-oriented), a word search can miss you, even though you did the job. Only write what you have actually done. Words you cannot stand behind do not belong in the CV.

Write both the short form and the full name the first time, for example "CRM (kundregister)".

### Hard facts

Company names, titles and years should be clear. If you have numbers for what you achieved and can stand behind them, include them.

## Checklist

- One column, no text boxes
- Common file format, preferably the one the ad asks for
- Standard headings
- Words from the ad where they match your experience
- Contact details as text
- The text can be copied in the right order

## What not to do

Hidden text in white color to fill the CV with keywords is a bad idea. A person who selects the page will see it, and what you want them to read is your real experience. The same goes for copying the ad word for word.

## Creative layout or simple?

A graphic CV can suit a creative role or a small company where one person reads it. If you apply through a form, it is easier to have a simple version ready. You do not have to choose between two CVs: the same content can exist in a simple and a more graphic layout. In the CV builder in Jobin you can change template without writing anything again.

## What do I do now

Open your CV, copy the text into Notepad and see if it makes sense. Then read the ad you are applying for and compare the words. ## Common questions

### What is an ATS system?

Software that an employer uses to receive, read and organize applications and candidates.

### Does ATS sort out my CV automatically?

It depends on how the employer has set up the system. The automatic sorting can come from screening questions in the form. You cannot know in advance what applies, so write a CV that is easy to read and answer the questions truthfully.

### How do I test that my CV works in an ATS?

Copy the text into Notepad and check the order. There are also services that say they check ATS-friendliness. It is not possible to know how well they match the systems that employers actually use, so see them as a rough guide.

### Do I need two different CVs?

No, but adjust the content for each ad. It is good to put the same content in a simple layout for forms.
