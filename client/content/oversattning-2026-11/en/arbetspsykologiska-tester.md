TITLE: Work psychology tests: examples and what they measure
SUMMARY: Examples of tasks in work psychology tests, ability and personality tests compared, and how to prepare without guessing the "right" answers.

Work psychology tests are tasks and questions that an employer or a recruitment company uses as input about how you solve problems and how you usually work. There are three kinds, and you prepare for them in different ways: ability tests, personality tests and work samples.

## Three kinds of tests and what each one tries to answer

People often mix them up, but they measure different things:

| Type of test | What it measures | What "good" means |
|---|---|---|
| Ability test (begåvningstest) | How you solve new problems: logic, numbers, words, patterns | The right answer within the time, not prior knowledge |
| Personality test | How you usually act: cooperation, accuracy, initiative | There is no right answer, only a profile |
| Work sample (arbetsprov) | How you solve a task that is like the job | How you think, not only the final result |

The employer decides which tests are used, when in the process, and how much they count. It is fine to ask. See further down. You can read how the test fits with the other steps in [The recruitment process in 7 steps](/knowledge-base/article/rekryteringsprocessen-steg-for-steg).

## Work psychology tests: examples of tasks

This is what the tasks can look like. The exact format differs between test providers.

- **Number series:** you get a series of numbers and decide which one comes next.
- **Word understanding and reading:** you read a short text and decide if a statement is true, is not true, or cannot be decided.
- **Figure logic and matrices:** you see a row of pictures with a pattern and choose the one that is missing.
- **Working memory:** you hold information in your head while you do something else, for example remember a series of numbers or rules while you solve a task.
- **Situation judgement:** you get a work scenario and choose how you would act.
- **Personality questions:** you choose a place on a scale for statements like "I would rather plan than improvise".

A work sample can be a practical task or a case. The line between a work sample and unpaid work is described in [Work samples and cases](/knowledge-base/article/arbetsprov-och-case) and in [Trial work without pay](/knowledge-base/article/provjobba-utan-lon).

### Screening tests

A screening test is used for a first selection, and is often shorter than a full test. The employer decides how the result is counted, so ask how it is used.

## What a personality test really measures

A personality test asks how you usually behave: how you make decisions, how you react to stress, how you cooperate. There is no answer key. The question is never "do you do the right thing" but "does this match how you are".

That is why it is tempting to answer the way you think the employer wants. This is seldom a good choice. Some personality tests ask the same thing in several ways to see if the answers fit together. And even if the test does not do this: a profile based on a role you are playing says nothing about you, either to the employer or to you.

The simplest advice: answer based on how you actually usually act. A profile that does not fit a certain role is information, for you too.

## How to prepare for an ability test

You cannot study the right answers the evening before, but you can remove the surprises:

- **Get to know the format.** Some test providers put practice tasks on their websites. Which ones are free varies, and a practice test is not the same as your own test.
- **Practice with a clock**, not only without one. Time is often part of what is measured.
- **Read the instructions calmly** before you start, even if the clock is running. Misunderstanding the type of question costs more than those seconds.
- **Sleep and eat as usual.** Concentration tasks go worse when you are tired.
- **You do not have to finish everything.** A test with a time limit is often made so that people do not have time for all the tasks. Move on if you get stuck.

## What you can ask about

Before or during the process you can ask to know:

- what the test measures and why it is relevant for the job
- how the result is used: is it decisive or one of several inputs
- if you can get feedback on your result
- how your information is handled and how long it is saved

The last one is supported by the data protection rules: whoever handles your personal data must be able to tell you what is saved and why. The [Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten (Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection)](https://www.imy.se) describes your rights.

## If the test situation does not suit you

If you have dyslexia, ADHD, test anxiety or something else that means the test does not show what you can do, contact the recruiter in advance and ask about adjustments: more time, another format or a calmer place. According to the [Diskrimineringsombudsmannen (Equality Ombudsman)](https://www.do.se), an employer must take reasonable measures for accessibility, and this also applies to you as a job seeker. What is reasonable is decided in each case, so a yes is not certain. But the question is reasonable. You can read more about what you can ask for in [Dyslexia in your application and at work](/knowledge-base/article/dyslexi-i-ansokan-och-pa-jobbet) and [ADHD in working life](/knowledge-base/article/adhd-i-arbetslivet).

## What do I do now

If you have a test booked: find out what kind of test it is and if the provider has practice tasks. Book a time when you are rested, if you can choose. Ask for feedback afterwards, whatever the result. If you want to practice for the interview that is part of the process, the interview simulator in Jobin can help.

## Common questions

### Can I say no to a test?

Ask the recruiter why the test is used and what happens if you opt out. What an employer does then is their decision, so do not expect that it makes no difference. If you have a reason, for example a disability, first bring up the question of adjustment.

### What is a work psychology screening test?

A test that is used early for a first selection, often shorter than a full test. Ask how the result is used.

### Are there free ability tests to practice on?

Some providers and other websites have practice tasks. Check yourself if they are free, and expect that they give a picture of the format, not of how your test will go.

### Are my test results saved?

You have the right to know what information is saved, why and for how long. Ask the employer or the recruitment company if it is not in the information you received.

### Does a bad result mean that I do not suit the job?

A result is input, and the employer decides how it is counted. The test measures one occasion and one profile, not everything you can do. Ask for feedback to understand what it pointed to.
