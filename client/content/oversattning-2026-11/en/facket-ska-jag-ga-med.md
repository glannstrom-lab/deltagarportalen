TITLE: Joining a union: what you get and what it costs
SUMMARY: What union membership gives, how to join, what decides the fee and the difference from a-kassa. How to find the right union for your job.

To join a union (facket) means that you become a member of a fackförbund (trade union) that negotiates terms with employers and supports you if there are problems at work. You join by applying to the union that organizes your job or your industry, usually on the union's website. What it costs is decided by the union.

## What is the difference between the union and a-kassa?

A **fackförbund** (trade union) is an organization for people who work in a certain job or a certain industry. The union negotiates with employers and supports its members in matters about their employment.

An **a-kassa** (unemployment insurance fund) is insurance against unemployment. If you are a member and become unemployed, you can, if you meet the conditions, get compensation while you look for a new job. The a-kassa decides this itself.

You do not become a member of one by joining the other. They are two applications, and you can choose only one of them, see [A-kassa without the union](/knowledge-base/article/a-kassa-utan-facket).

## What does union membership give?

It varies between unions, but what is mentioned is:

- **Support in a dispute.** If there are problems with the employer, for example around a notice of termination, the union can represent you.
- **Negotiation**, both in individual matters such as your pay and in the negotiations that set the frame for the workplace.
- **Advice** about employment terms, work environment and rights.
- **Sometimes extra insurance**, for example an income insurance that adds to a-kassa.

The value is seen most when something goes wrong. Check what your own union offers, because it is not the same everywhere.

## The union or a-kassa, or both?

| | Fackförbund (trade union) | A-kassa |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Support and negotiation about your employment | Insurance against unemployment |
| Gives compensation when unemployed? | No, but may have an extra insurance | Yes, if you meet the conditions |
| Support in a conflict at work? | Yes | No |

If you want both, you need to apply for both. How a-kassa works is described in [How a-kassa works](/knowledge-base/article/a-kassa-sa-fungerar-det). Easy Swedish is in [What is the union?](/knowledge-base/article/lattsvenska-facket).

## How do I join a union?

1. **Find out which union organizes your industry.** Ask colleagues, or look at the unions' websites. The unions belong to the three main organizations [LO](https://www.lo.se), [TCO](https://www.tco.se) and [Saco](https://www.saco.se), and some stand outside.
2. **Apply for membership** with the union, usually through a form on its website.
3. **Pay the membership fee**, according to the union's terms.

Also check if your workplace has a kollektivavtal (collective agreement), because it affects which terms apply at work. See [Kollektivavtal: what is it?](/knowledge-base/article/kollektivavtal-vad-det-betyder).

## What does it cost to be in a union?

Membership costs a running fee. How large it is and how it is worked out, for example as a fixed sum or in relation to your pay, is decided by each union. Some unions have a different fee during your studies or when you are unemployed, but that is in the union's own terms. There is no amount that applies to all, so look at the union you are thinking about. The union fee is separate from the fee to a-kassa.

Weigh the cost against what you get. If the workplace is your first, if the industry is new to you, or if the security around a possible dispute feels important, then the support weighs most. It is your decision, and there is no answer that applies to everyone.

## Do you have to be in a union?

No, it is your choice. If the employer has a kollektivavtal, the terms usually apply to you too even if you are not a member. Ask the employer if you want to know what applies to you. What you do not get as a non-member is what the union offers its members, such as legal support in a dispute.

## How long do you have to be a member to get help?

That is decided by the union's terms. Some benefits may require a certain time as a member, others do not. Ask the union before you need the help, not after.

## What do I do now?

Find out which union organizes your industry and read what their membership gives. Then decide separately if you want an a-kassa, and which one. Apply for what you choose directly with each organization.

## Common questions

### Do I have to be in a union to get a-kassa?
No. They are two separate memberships that you apply for separately.

### Am I automatically in a-kassa if I join a union?
No, they are two applications. Ask the union if there is a connected a-kassa and how you join it.

### Can I change union if I change industry?
Yes, you can end one membership and apply to another union. Check the terms for leaving at the union you leave.

### What does it cost to be in a union?
It is decided by the union and can differ between unions and membership categories. Look at the union's website.
