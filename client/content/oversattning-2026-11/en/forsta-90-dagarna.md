TITLE: 30-60-90 day plan: your first 90 days at a new job
SUMMARY: A template for a 30-60-90 day plan: what to do in the first, second and third month at a new job, and how to adapt the plan to your role.

A 30-60-90 day plan divides your first three months at a new job into three parts: learn, contribute and establish yourself. It is a planning tool that you can use by yourself or make together with your manager. It is not a demand from the employer. Here is a template to start from, and a way to adapt it to your job.

## Why divide the first 90 days?

The start of a new job has a lot of new things at once: people, systems, routines and expectations. A plan helps you know what to focus on right now and what can wait. The numbers 30, 60 and 90 are a way to divide the time, not a rule. Your employer may have its own introduction, and your employment may have a probation period (prövotid) that decides other things, see [Så fungerar provanställning](/knowledge-base/article/provanstallning-guide) (how probationary employment works).

## Day 1–30: Learn and listen

**Main task:** take in information. You do not yet know what you do not know.

### The first week

**Practical things:**
- Learn the systems and tools you need to be able to work.
- Understand your role and what is expected of you.
- Go through the introduction material.

**Relationships:**
- Introduce yourself to the people you meet.
- Ask your manager who you should talk to and ask for short talks.
- Find someone who can explain the unwritten rules.

### The rest of the month

**Understand the business:**
- What does the company or organization do, and for whom?
- What are the most important goals and challenges?

**Understand your role:**
- What should you deliver, and how can you tell that it is going well?
- Who decides what, and who do you ask about what?

Questions are welcome when you are new. Say yes to fika (a coffee break with colleagues) and lunch when you have the energy for it.

## Day 31–60: Contribute and deliver

**Main task:** start taking your own tasks and deliver them.

- Find a smaller thing you can improve or take over. It is often a good start.
- Deepen the relationships with the people you work with most.
- Ask for a check-in with your manager: How is it going? What should I focus more or less on? What do I need to learn?
- Deliver what you have promised. Say early if you will not have time.
- Ask for feedback regularly. Admit mistakes when they happen.

## Day 61–90: Deepen and establish

**Main task:** take more responsibility where you feel secure.

- Take on bigger tasks, or run a smaller part yourself.
- Suggest improvements when you understand why things are done the way they are.
- Prepare a talk with your manager: what have you done, what is going well, what is the next goal, what do you want to develop in.
- Write down what you have achieved so that you remember it.

## Template: 30-60-90 day plan

**Day 1–30: learn**
- Understand the role and the expectations
- Meet the most important people
- Learn the systems and routines
- Find someone to ask

**Day 31–60: contribute**
- Deliver your first task
- Book a check-in with your manager
- Deepen the relationships
- Ask for feedback

**Day 61–90: establish**
- Take more of your own responsibility
- Suggest an improvement
- Book a follow-up talk
- Set the next goal

Change it to fit the job. In some jobs it takes more than 90 days to settle in, and in others it goes faster. The plan is there to help you, not to judge you.

## New at work and the only new person

Are you alone in the role, or is there no one to show you around? Ask your manager for a short plan for the first weeks: who you should talk to, which tasks are yours and when you check in. Write down what you agreed, so that you have something to go back to.

## Are you a new manager?

An introduction for a new manager is the same idea on a bigger scale. Think about:

- getting to know the employees one by one before you change anything
- understanding how the business is measured and what goals you have
- finding out what your own manager expects, and when you follow up
- not changing too much before you have understood why things are the way they are
- telling the team how you want to work and asking how they want it

This is a checklist to start from, not an answer key.

## Common mistakes

- Trying to change a lot too fast.
- Comparing everything with your last job.
- Sitting alone at the desk and not asking.
- Promising more than you have time for.
- Avoiding feedback.
- Forgetting to write down what you have learned and achieved.

## What do I do now

Write down three things you want to have done after the first month and book a check-in with your manager. If it is your first day, read [Första dagen på nya jobbet](/knowledge-base/article/forsta-dagen-nytt-jobb) (the first day at the new job). If you want to understand the terms of your employment, see [Så läser du ett anställningsavtal](/knowledge-base/article/las-anstallningsavtal) (how to read an employment contract). How to talk to colleagues and your manager is in [Professionell kommunikation på arbetsplatsen](/knowledge-base/article/professionell-kommunikation) (professional communication at work).

## Common questions

### What is a 30-60-90 day plan?

A plan that divides the first three months at a new job into learn, contribute and establish yourself.

### Do I have to make a 30-60-90 day plan?

No, it is a tool you can use. Ask your manager if there is an introduction or a plan.

### Which steps should be in an onboarding checklist for a new manager?

See the section "Are you a new manager?" above. The most important things are to get to know the team, understand the goals and check expectations with your own manager.
