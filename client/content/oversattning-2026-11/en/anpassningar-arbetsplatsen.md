TITLE: Workplace adjustments: examples of adjustments at work
SUMMARY: Examples of workplace adjustments for a disability, what the law says and how to bring it up with your manager.

Workplace adjustment (arbetsanpassning) means changes in the work environment, the work tasks or the working hours that let you do your job with a disability or a health challenge. Examples are a desk that can be raised and lowered, a quieter place, written instructions or more flexible working hours. Here are examples, what the law says and how to bring it up.

## Examples of workplace adjustments

What helps depends on you and on the work. These are examples, not a list you can demand from:

- **Physical environment:** a desk that can be raised and lowered, a special chair, accessible rooms, a parking space near the entrance.
- **Technology:** screen readers, speech synthesis, magnifying software, a hearing loop in meeting rooms, visual signals instead of sound.
- **Working time and place:** adjusted working hours, regular breaks, the possibility to work from home if the work allows it.
- **Sound and interruptions:** a quieter place, headphones or ear protection, fewer interruptions.
- **Information and structure:** written instructions instead of spoken ones, clear planning, fixed structure.
- **Work tasks:** changed or redistributed tasks, help with heavy lifting.
- **Contact and follow-up:** regular contact with the manager about how it works.

There is no adjustment that belongs to a certain diagnosis. Two people with the same diagnosis can need completely different things.

## What the law says

Two laws are important.

**The Work Environment Act (arbetsmiljölagen).** The employer is responsible for the work environment. The [Arbetsmiljöverket (Swedish Work Environment Authority)](https://www.av.se) writes that the employer must continuously find out if someone needs workplace adjustment, and that you should be able to take part when the adjustment is investigated, designed and followed up. Arbetsmiljöverket also writes that the law does not say how far an employer must go.

**The Discrimination Act (diskrimineringslagen).** The [Diskrimineringsombudsmannen (DO, the Equality Ombudsman)](https://www.do.se) describes that lack of accessibility is a form of discrimination. This means that an employer must take reasonable measures for accessibility. What is reasonable is decided in each case, and DO cannot give a general list. DO mentions, among other things, the employer's financial and practical conditions and how lasting and wide the relationship is. A job is an example of a lasting and wide relationship.

This means that the right is not unlimited, but that an employer cannot dismiss the question without having looked at it.

## How to bring it up with the employer

1. **Prepare.** Write down what is difficult and what would help. The more concrete, the easier it is to answer. Bring a certificate or investigation if you have one.
2. **Contact the right person.** Your closest manager, HR or your skyddsombud (safety representative).
3. **Have the talk.** You do not have to tell everything. What the employer needs to know is which adjustments you need, not necessarily the diagnosis. Suggest concrete solutions and be open to others.
4. **Write down what you agreed.**
5. **Follow up.** Try for a while and adjust if it does not work.

An example of wording: "I find it hard to concentrate in an open office. Can we talk about a quieter place or about me using headphones?"

## Support from Arbetsförmedlingen and Försäkringskassan

The employer can get help. [Arbetsförmedlingen (the Swedish Public Employment Service)](https://arbetsformedlingen.se) has grants for work aids, personal assistant at work and lönebidrag (wage subsidy), and, at the start of a job, support from a support person. Försäkringskassan (the Swedish Social Insurance Agency) can pay for work aids after some time in a job. Read about them here:

- [Grants for work aids](/knowledge-base/article/arbetshjalpmedel-pa-jobbet)
- [Personal assistant at work](/knowledge-base/article/personligt-bitrade)
- [Lönebidrag](/knowledge-base/article/lonebidrag-sa-fungerar-det)
- [SIUS: support when you are introduced to a new job](/knowledge-base/article/sius-stod-vid-introduktion)

This can make it easier for an employer to say yes.

## If the employer says no

1. Ask what the obstacle is. Often another solution can be found.
2. Ask the union if you are a member.
3. You can contact DO, who can answer if it may be discrimination.
4. Talk to your case officer at Arbetsförmedlingen about whether support exists.

More about discrimination is in [Discrimination when job searching](/knowledge-base/article/diskriminering-jobbsokning).

## What do I do now

Write down three things that make the work day difficult and what would help for each one. Then book a time with your manager.

## Common questions

### Do I have to tell about my disability?
You have no duty to tell. But for the employer to be able to adjust, they need to know what you need.

### Can I get adjustments during trial employment?
The Discrimination Act and the Work Environment Act also apply if you are on trial employment. Read more in [How trial employment works](/knowledge-base/article/provanstallning-guide).

### What is the difference from work aids?
A work aid is something you personally need. An adjustment can also be changed routines or a different place. They can be combined.

### How can I adjust my office?
Start with what disturbs you most, for example sound, light or sitting position. Ask for a solution for that first.
