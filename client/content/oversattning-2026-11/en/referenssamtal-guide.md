TITLE: Reference call: questions, timing and how to prepare
SUMMARY: When references are contacted, which questions are asked, and how you ask and prepare your references. With a template for the message.

A reference call is when an employer phones or emails someone you have named, to hear what it was like to work with you. Here you learn when references are usually contacted, which questions are asked when taking references, and how you prepare yourself and them.

## What is a reference call?

References are people who can tell how you work. The employer uses them to check that the picture you gave in the application and at the interview is correct. The call is most often by phone, sometimes by email.

## When are references contacted?

The employer decides. Often it happens after the interviews and before an offer is made. If someone asks for references, it can be a sign of interest, but it is not a promise of the job.

You can ask the recruiter when the references will be contacted and which ones are to be included. Then you do not have to guess.

## Who can be your reference?

Choose people who have seen you work and who can speak about it.

**Good references:**

- A former manager
- A colleague who worked closely with you
- A customer or partner
- A supervisor from a work placement, work training or education

**Less good:**

- Relatives and close friends who have not seen you work
- Someone you had a conflict with
- Someone who does not remember you

### If you do not have much work experience

It is fine to name others who know your work:

- Supervisors from a work placement, [work training](/knowledge-base/article/arbetstraning-vad-innebar-det) or education
- Teachers
- Someone who led an association you were part of
- Someone you helped as a volunteer

Help with choosing is in [References: how to choose and prepare them](/knowledge-base/article/referenser-guide).

### If you have been away for a long time or the job ended badly

Choose a person who can talk about the work itself, even if the job ended on a bad note. It is good if you tell, briefly and yourself, what happened, before anyone else does. Read [Why did you leave your last job?](/knowledge-base/article/varfor-slutade-du-pa-forra-jobbet).

## Always ask first

Call or write to the person before you name them. Ask if they want to and have the possibility. Then tell them:

- Which job you have applied for and what it involves
- What you highlighted in the interview
- Roughly when the call may come
- How they can best be reached

It is good to send the job ad and your CV. Then the reference can answer in a way that fits.

**This is what a message can sound like:** "Hello, I have come some way in a recruitment for a position as [title] and wonder if I may name you as a reference. The job is about [short description]. I will send the job ad and my CV. Please contact me if you have questions."

## Which questions are asked when taking references?

The questions are about what it was like to work with you. Typical questions:

**About your relationship**
- How do you know the person, and how long did you work together?
- What role and responsibility did the person have?

**About the work**
- How would you describe how the person works?
- What are the person's strengths?
- Is there something the person can develop in?

**About cooperation**
- How did the person work in the group?
- How did the person handle a stressful period?

**About leadership, if the job requires it**
- How did the person lead others?

**Closing**
- Would you hire the person again?
- Is there anything more we should know?

Think about how you yourself would answer these questions. It is also a good exercise for [strengths and weaknesses in an interview](/knowledge-base/article/styrkor-svagheter-intervju).

## Getting an answer after reference calls: what happens next?

After the calls the employer weighs together everything they have learned. How long it takes and how they get in touch varies, and it can depend on several things besides the references.

- If the recruiter has said when you will get an answer, wait for that time.
- If the time has passed without an answer, get in touch in a friendly way and ask how it is going.
- If you get a rejection, you can ask for feedback, see [Feedback after a rejection](/knowledge-base/article/feedback-efter-avslag).
- You cannot always find out what a reference has said.

A rejection after taking references does not mean that a reference said something negative. There can be other reasons.

## Your information and your rights

Taking references is about personal data. You have the right to ask the employer which information about you they have collected and how they use it. Guidance is available from the [Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten (Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection)](https://www.imy.se). And if you feel that you were treated unfairly in a recruitment, see [Discrimination in job searching](/knowledge-base/article/diskriminering-jobbsokning).

## Common questions

### Do I have to name references?
The employer can ask for them, but you decide which ones you name. You can also ask them to make contact only when you have received an offer, if you do not want your current employer to know that you are applying.

### Can they contact my current manager?
Ask the recruiter in good time and say clearly what you want. You can ask them to wait until you have approved it.

### How many references should I have?
It depends on the employer. Have a few ready in advance, so that you can answer when someone asks.

### What is a referent?
A referent (referent) is the person who gives the reference, that is, the person the employer calls.

### How long after an interview does the call come?
It is not possible to say. Ask the recruiter what the process looks like.

## What do I do now

Write down the names and contact details of a few people who can be references. Get in touch with them already now, even before you need them. If you get a job, say thank you to the references afterwards. More tips are at [Arbetsförmedlingen (the Swedish Public Employment Service)](https://arbetsformedlingen.se).
