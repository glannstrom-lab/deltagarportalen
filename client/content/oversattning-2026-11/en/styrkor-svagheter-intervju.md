TITLE: Strengths and weaknesses in an interview: sample answers
SUMMARY: Sample answers about strengths and weaknesses, what you can say about three bad qualities, and how to avoid clichés in a job interview.

The question about strengths and weaknesses is a classic in interviews, and you can prepare for it. Here you get sample answers, a simple model for choosing weaknesses to mention, and a guide to what you can answer to "three bad qualities".

## What the interviewer really wants to know

The question is not a trap. The person interviewing you wants to understand four things:

- Do you know yourself well enough to describe what you are good at?
- Are you honest, or are you repeating something you practiced?
- Do you work on what is hard for you?
- Do your strengths fit this particular job?

So you do not need to be perfect. You need to show that you know how you work.

## How to answer the question about strengths

Choose two or three strengths that fit the job. Read the ad and see what it asks for, and choose what you can really stand behind.

Do not only say what you are. Give a short example, preferably with the [STAR method](/knowledge-base/article/kompetensbaserad-intervju-star-metoden): situation, task, action and result.

**Weak answer:** "I am driven and good at working together."

**Strong answer:** "One of my strengths is that I keep order when many things are going on at the same time. In my last job the deliveries started to slip, so I suggested a short check-in at the start of every week. After that everyone knew what was most important, and we caught up. I think that ability would help me in this role, where several tasks run in parallel."

### Sample answers for different jobs

- **Shop:** "I am calm when it gets crowded. When the queue grew, I took one customer at a time and told those waiting that I would be with them soon. That kept the mood good."
- **Warehouse:** "I am careful. I check the picking list one more time before I close the package, and that has reduced the mistakes in my area."
- **Health care and care work:** "I listen. I often notice when someone wants to say something but cannot get it out, and I give them time."

If you have no work experience, your example can come from a work placement, studies, club or association life, or everyday life. It counts.

## How to answer the question about weaknesses

This is the part that feels hardest. A model in three steps makes it easier:

1. **Choose a real weakness.** The answer "I am a perfectionist" says little about you, because anyone can use it.
2. **Choose something that is not decisive for the job.** If you apply as a salesperson, do not say that you dislike customer contact.
3. **Tell what you do about it.** That is the part that shows development.

**Example:** "I have found it hard to ask for help, because I wanted to manage everything myself. I have noticed that it takes longer, so now I ask earlier when I get stuck. It gets better each time."

## Three bad qualities in an interview: what can you say?

If the question is asked as "name three bad qualities", you can pick from this list. Choose the ones that are true for you and that do not clash with the job.

- **Hard to say no.** Explain that you now prioritize and say so when something cannot be done in time.
- **Nervous before presentations.** Tell that you practice beforehand and take some time to prepare.
- **Impatient when things go slowly.** Show that you try to find something else to do while waiting.
- **Focused on details.** Describe how you set a limit for how long you spend on one detail.
- **Uncomfortable with small talk.** Say that you are better at talking about the work itself and that you are practicing the everyday chat.
- **Hard to delegate.** Tell how you have started to hand over tasks and trust others.

All three do not need to be big. Better three small and honest ones than one that sounds made up.

## Weaknesses not to mention

- **Something that stops you in the job.** "I find it hard to be on time" is an answer to a different question than the one asked.
- **Something very private.** Stay with working life.
- **"I have no weaknesses."** That gives the impression that you have not thought about it.

This is also true if you have a diagnosis or a disability. You decide yourself if and when you tell about it, see [To tell about a diagnosis or not](/knowledge-base/article/beratta-om-diagnos-eller-inte).

## Prepare at home

1. Write down five strengths. Choose the three that fit the job best.
2. Find a short example for each.
3. Write down three weaknesses. Choose one or two that you can talk about calmly.
4. Write what you do about them.
5. Say the answers out loud. You can practice in the [interview simulator](/interview-simulator).

The question is often connected to other questions: [Tell me about yourself](/knowledge-base/article/beratta-om-dig-sjalv) and [Why should we hire you?](/knowledge-base/article/varfor-ska-vi-anstalla-dig). If you use the same examples in several answers, it sounds more like a whole.

## Common questions

### What are your strengths?
Answer with two or three strengths that fit the job, and give a short example of where you have shown them. See the sample answers above.

### What are my strengths if I do not know?
Ask someone who knows you or has worked with you. Also look at what people usually thank you for. There is help in [Discover your strengths](/knowledge-base/article/upptack-dina-styrkor).

### How many weaknesses should I mention?
Answer what is asked. If the question is in the singular, one is enough. If they ask for several, give several, short ones.

### May I say that I am nervous?
Yes, if it is true and you show what you do about it. It is an understandable answer.

## What do I do now

Write down your three strengths and one weakness, with one example each. Read them out loud once, and change what sounds strange. More help before the interview is at [Arbetsförmedlingen](https://arbetsformedlingen.se) (the Swedish Public Employment Service).
