TITLE: Phone interview: questions you get and how to answer
SUMMARY: Common questions in a phone interview, how to think when you answer, and what to do before and after the call. Tips for short and long calls.

A phone interview is a call where a recruiter phones to hear more about you, often before you meet in person. Here you get the questions you often meet, how to think when you answer, and what you can do to make the call feel calmer.

## What is a phone interview, and why do they call?

Companies use phone interviews to get a first picture of you without having to book a meeting. Sometimes it is a short phone interview where the recruiter checks a few things. Sometimes it is a longer interview that replaces a first meeting.

The recruiter listens for:

- How you tell about yourself
- If you have understood what the job is about
- If your expectations and the job match
- Practical things such as when you can start

The call can come unexpectedly. Sometimes it is booked in advance. Sometimes someone calls and asks if you have time right now. You can say no and ask for another time.

## Before the call

- Read the job ad and your own CV once more. Have them in front of you.
- Write a few keywords about yourself, but do not read out a text.
- Write down two or three questions you want to ask.
- Find a place that is quiet and where the phone has good signal.
- Have paper and a pen, and a glass of water.

If the call comes without warning, it is quite reasonable to say: "Hello, I am glad you called. Can we talk in a moment so that I can sit down somewhere?"

## Common questions in a phone interview, and how to think

### Tell me about yourself
The recruiter wants a short version, not your whole life story. Start with what you do or have done, mention what fits the job, and end with why you are applying. There is more help in [Tell me about yourself](/knowledge-base/article/beratta-om-dig-sjalv).

### Why are you applying for this job?
Mention something you have really read about the company or the tasks. It shows that you have thought about it. See also [Why do you want to work with us?](/knowledge-base/article/varfor-vill-du-jobba-hos-oss).

### What do you know about us?
You do not need to know everything. Say two or three things you found on the website or in the ad.

### What did you do in your last job?
Describe the tasks in short and highlight what is similar to the new job.

### Why did you leave?
Be short and factual and do not speak badly about anyone. You will find a guide in [Why did you leave your last job?](/knowledge-base/article/varfor-slutade-du-pa-forra-jobbet).

### Tell me about a time when you solved a problem
This is a competency-based question. Answer in steps: situation, task, action, result. See [The STAR method](/knowledge-base/article/kompetensbaserad-intervju-star-metoden).

### What are your strengths and weaknesses?
Prepare one or two of each, with an example. See [strengths and weaknesses in an interview](/knowledge-base/article/styrkor-svagheter-intervju).

### What are your salary expectations?
Find out what similar jobs usually pay before the call, for example through a union or salary statistics. Help is available under [Salary](/salary) in the portal and in [Salary requirements in your application](/knowledge-base/article/lonekrav-i-ansokan).

### When can you start?
Think through your notice period or other things that decide, and have an honest answer.

### Do you have any questions for us?
Say yes. It is good to have asked questions, for example what the work day looks like, what the next step is and when you can get an answer. More ideas are in [Questions to ask the employer](/knowledge-base/article/fragor-att-stalla-till-arbetsgivaren).

## During the call

Voice and tone do the work that body language does otherwise.

- Say your name when you answer.
- Sit or stand straight. It can make your voice clearer.
- Speak calmly, and take a pause before you answer if you need to.
- It is fine to say "good question, I need a second to think".
- Ask if you cannot hear or understand. "Can you repeat the question?" is completely okay.
- Let the recruiter finish talking before you answer.

If the line breaks, wait a little while and call back if you have the number. Otherwise write a short message or an email.

## If no one calls

If you have booked a time and no one calls, wait a little while and then contact the person who booked. Do it in a friendly and short way.

## Phone or video?

Some interviews are held as video calls instead. Then partly the same things apply, but also picture and light. Read [Video interview: how to do well in front of the camera](/knowledge-base/article/videointervju-tips) and [Digital interview](/knowledge-base/article/digital-intervju).

## After the call

- Write down what was said and which next steps were mentioned.
- You can send a short thank-you. See [Thank-you letter after an interview](/knowledge-base/article/tackbrev-intervju).
- If you have not received an answer when the recruiter said it would come, you can get in touch and ask how it is going.

## Common questions

### How long does a phone interview last?
It varies. A short call can be a check of a few things, and a longer one can be like a regular interview. You can ask in advance how much time is set aside.

### Do I have to answer right away if they call unexpectedly?
No. You can ask for another time. It is fine to say that you need a quieter place.

### Can I have notes in front of me?
Yes. That is one of the advantages of the phone. Use keywords, and do not read out whole answers.

### What do I do if I find it hard to hear or speak on the phone?
You can ask for another form or an adjustment. Read more in [Adjustments at the workplace](/knowledge-base/article/anpassningar-arbetsplats) and at [Arbetsförmedlingen (the Swedish Public Employment Service)](https://arbetsformedlingen.se).

## What do I do now

Choose three questions from the list above and say the answers out loud, preferably in the [interview simulator](/interview-simulator). Put the phone on charge and keep your CV close, and you will be ready when it rings.
