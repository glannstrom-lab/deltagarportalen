TITLE: Background check: what may the employer check?
SUMMARY: What a background check can include, how to order an extract from Polisen, and what the employer may do with the extract.

A background check is when an employer checks information about you before they hire you, for example through references, certificates or a register extract. What is allowed depends on the job. For some jobs an extract from the belastningsregistret (the criminal record register) is a requirement, for others the employer may not demand it. You order the extract yourself from Polisen (the Swedish Police Authority).

## What a background check can include

The most common checks are things you take part in yourself: references you have given, grades and certificates, and the employer checking that the information in your CV is correct. An employer may process your personal data only if the data protection rules allow it, and the more sensitive the information is, the clearer the link to the job needs to be. You can ask what is checked and why. That is a reasonable question.

It is hard to stop a recruiter from googling your name or looking at a public profile. More systematic checking of what is not public, or has nothing to do with the job, is a matter for Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten (IMY, the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection). If you think the check is about discrimination, you can contact [Diskrimineringsombudsmannen](https://www.do.se) (the Equality Ombudsman).

## Background check, free of charge, and the police

What Polisen offers you is a register extract from the belastnings- och misstankeregistret (the criminal record and suspicion register). [Polisen](https://polisen.se) states that the extract is free of charge, except when it is to be used abroad. Check Polisen's website to see if this is still true.

There are different extracts for different jobs, for example work in school and preschool, work with children in other activities, work at an HVB-hem (a home for care or residence), work in the home with older people or adults with a disability, and insurance distribution. Choose the extract that matches the job, and ask the employer if you are unsure which one. Which extract to choose is also the question behind "which belastningsregister should I choose".

## What is shown in the belastningsregistret

The register is now called the belastnings- och misstankeregistret. Polisen describes that it contains information about whether you are reasonably suspected of, or have received penalties for, crimes. What is shown in an extract depends on what kind of extract it is. An extract for work in school shows different information than one for work in the home of older people. Polisen has changed what the school extract covers from July 2026, so read Polisen's page for the extract you are going to use.

## Who demands an extract, and when do you show it

- For some jobs the employer can demand the extract. Polisen's page says which. Polisen does not send the extract directly to the employer. You get it and show it, and Polisen's page says what the employer may do with it.
- If your job is not on Polisen's list, the employer may not demand that you show an extract. You can show one voluntarily.
- Never include the extract in a job application. Polisen says that you show it only after you have received a job offer.

## May you read it first, and what may the employer do with it

You have the right to read your extract before the employer does, and there is no requirement to show it in an unopened envelope. This is stated on Polisen's page for employers.

What the employer may do with the extract depends on what kind of extract it is:

- For school and child care, other work with children, and work in the home of older people or adults with a disability, the employer may note that the extract was shown, but may not keep or copy it.
- For HVB-hem and LSS activities for children (LSS is the Swedish law on support and service for people with certain disabilities), the employer must instead save the extract or a copy.
- The employer must give back the original if you ask for it.

So it is not correct to say that "the employer may never save it". It depends on the job.

## If you have an entry in the register

An entry can matter when it is connected to the job, for example a financial crime and a role with responsibility for money. An entry without that connection weighs differently, and what is shown in an extract depends on the type of extract. Ask the employer or the union (facket) if you are unsure how an entry is judged for this particular job.

### Bringing it up yourself

If you know that the extract will be shown, it can be good to tell it yourself before it is asked for. Then you can control how it is said. You can keep it short: what happened, about when, and what is different now. You do not need to defend yourself in detail. Which timing is best depends on the job and the process.

## What do I do now

If it is a job that requires an extract: order the right kind of extract in good time. Read it yourself first. Show it when you have received an offer. If you are unsure whether a check is reasonable, ask why it is done.

## Common questions

### Can an employer demand an extract for any job?
No. For some jobs there are requirements in law. If the job is not on Polisen's list, the employer may not demand an extract.

### May an employer save my register extract?
It depends on the job. For some extracts the employer may only note that it was shown. For others, such as HVB-hem, the employer must save it.

### Do I have to hand in the extract unopened?
No. You have the right to read it first, and there is no requirement for an unopened envelope, according to Polisen.

### What is shown in the belastningsregistret?
It depends on what kind of extract you order. Polisen's page describes each kind.

### Is a background check of social media allowed?
Looking at what is public is hard to stop. More systematic checks of other things are a matter for IMY. Ask the employer what is checked and why.

### What happens if I say nothing and it comes out anyway?
If the entry comes out in an extract that you are going to show anyway, the employer has already seen it. Then it may have been better to bring it up yourself, so that you could tell it in your own words.
