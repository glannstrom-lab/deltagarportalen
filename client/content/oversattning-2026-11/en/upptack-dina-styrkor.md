TITLE: What are your strengths? How to find them
SUMMARY: What strengths mean, four questions to find your own, exercises to do at your own pace, and how to talk about them in an interview.

Your strengths are the things you do well and that also give you energy, for example organizing, explaining, solving problems or listening. If you want to know what your strengths are, you can start with the four questions further down. No one but you can see the whole picture, but other people can help you see more.

## What do strengths mean?

In this guide we use the word strengths in a special way: it is something you do well and that gives you energy. The word is used in other ways too, but this definition is useful when you look for a job, because it points to what you want to do more of.

### The difference between strengths and skills

- **Skills** are things you have learned to do, for example drive a car or use Excel.
- **Strengths** are things you do well and get energy from, for example organizing, solving problems or motivating others.

You can be skilled at something without it being a strength, and the other way around.

### Why is it good to know your strengths?

- **When you look for a job**, you can aim for roles where you get to do more of what you are good at.
- **In an interview**, it is easier to talk about yourself with examples, see [Styrkor och svagheter på intervju](/knowledge-base/article/styrkor-svagheter-intervju) (strengths and weaknesses in an interview).
- **At work**, you know what to put your energy into.

## What are my strengths? Four questions to start with

### 1. Look back at times that went well

Think of situations where you:
- felt engaged and lost track of time
- got thanks or appreciation from others
- made something work without it feeling heavy

### 2. Ask other people

Ask a few people who know you to answer:
- What do you think I am good at?
- When do you see me most engaged?
- What would you ask me for help with?

### 3. Notice what gives and takes energy

During one week: write down which tasks give you energy and which take it away.

### 4. Do an exercise

In Jobin there is an exercise called "Dina starkaste egenskaper" (your strongest qualities) among the exercises.

If it is hard to see anything yourself, others can see things you miss. Ask someone you trust for help.

## Exercise 1: Success stories

Choose three situations where you felt satisfied with what you did. They can be small: a school project, a hobby, an everyday situation.

For each one, answer on paper or in the diary:
- What did you do?
- What obstacles did you meet, and how did you solve them?
- How did it feel afterwards?
- Which strengths did you use?

Then look at all three: which strengths come back?

## Exercise 2: Energy log

During one week, write down every day one activity that gave you energy and one that took energy. After the week: what patterns do you see, and what do they say about your strengths?

## Exercise 3: Questions to others

Send a message to a few people:

"Hi, I am working on finding out my strengths and would appreciate your help. Can you answer three questions? 1. What do you think I am good at? 2. When have you seen me most engaged? 3. If you were to recommend me for a job, what would you say?"

Put the answers together and look for what comes back.

## Exercise 4: Your strengths map

List all the strengths you have found and sort them into groups: social, thinking, practical and creative. Then choose the five that feel most like you.

## Examples of strengths

These are examples to give you ideas, not a complete list.

**Social:** building trust, explaining clearly, working together, understanding other people's needs, helping people in conflicts, motivating others.

**Thinking:** analyzing information, seeing patterns, learning quickly, solving problems, seeing the whole picture, weighing facts.

**Practical:** organizing, planning, being careful, carrying out and finishing, keeping several things going, improving routines.

**Creative:** coming up with new solutions, seeing possibilities, visualizing, experimenting, adapting.

### What a strength can look like in everyday life

| Strength | How it can show | Roles where it can be used |
|--------|---------------------|-----------------------------|
| Organizing | You create order and systems | Administration, project coordination, planning |
| Empathy | You understand how others feel | Health care, customer service, personnel work, coaching |
| Analysis | You see patterns in information | Analysis, finance, investigation |
| Communication | You explain difficult things simply | Teaching, sales, communication |
| Problem solving | You find ways around obstacles | Technology, development, troubleshooting |
| Leadership | You get others to work together | Team leading, training, supervision |
| Creativity | You come up with new ideas | Design, marketing, product development |
| Perseverance | You keep going when it gets hard | Sales, research, running your own business |

The table is for inspiration. A strength can be used in many more roles than the ones listed here. If you want to explore occupations, you can try the interest guide in Jobin.

## How to talk about your strengths in an interview

The question often comes: "What are your strengths?" Answer in three steps:

1. Name the strength.
2. Give a concrete example with the STAR method: situation, task, what you did and what happened. See [STAR-metoden](/knowledge-base/article/kompetensbaserad-intervju-star-metoden) (the STAR method).
3. Connect it to the job you are applying for.

Here is an example of what an answer can look like, with your own details in square brackets:

"One of my strengths is organizing. At [workplace or situation] I was given the task of [task]. I [what you did], and the result was [what happened, without exaggerating]. I think that strength is useful in this role because [connection to the job ad]."

More wording and example answers are in [Styrkor och svagheter på intervju](/knowledge-base/article/styrkor-svagheter-intervju) (strengths and weaknesses in an interview) and [Personliga egenskaper – exempel till CV och intervju](/knowledge-base/article/personliga-egenskaper-exempel) (personal qualities, examples for CV and interview).

### Avoid

- Being too modest: "I am okay at communication, I guess..."
- Being too general: "I am good at everything."
- Saying things without examples.
- Choosing strengths that do not belong to the job.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up what you can do with what gives you energy
- Underestimating everyday strengths
- Comparing yourself with others instead of looking at yourself
- Not asking others
- Ignoring strengths because they "do not count" in working life

## What do I do now

1. Do exercise 1 or 2, which need the least energy.
2. Ask two people for their view.
3. Choose five strengths and write an example for each.
4. Use them in your CV and letter, and prepare answers for the interview.
5. Look at job ads and ask yourself which ones sound like something you would get to do more of. Help is in [Kompetensinventering – kartlägg allt du kan](/knowledge-base/article/kompetensinventering-guide) (skills inventory: map everything you can do) and [Identifiera och kommunicera dina styrkor](/knowledge-base/article/styrkor-svagheter) (identify and communicate your strengths).

## Common questions

### What are your strengths?

The answer is unique to you, but you can find it by looking back at what went well, asking others and noticing what gives you energy.

### What do strengths mean?

Something you do well, and often get energy from. Skills are things you have learned. Strengths are closer to how you work.

### What are my strengths if I do not know?

Start by asking two people and writing down three times that went well. The pattern can appear in how others describe you.

### Can I have a strength without having worked with it?

Yes. Strengths can show in school, associations, at home and in your free time. Write down examples from there.
