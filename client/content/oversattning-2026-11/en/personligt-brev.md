TITLE: Cover letter: how to write a good application letter
SUMMARY: How do you write a cover letter? Step by step, with examples of openings and endings and three complete letters to start from.

A cover letter, sometimes called an application letter, is what you send with your CV. It shows why you are applying for the job and why you fit. Here is how to write a cover letter step by step, with examples of an opening, a middle part and an ending, and with three complete letters to start from.

## What is a cover letter, and what should it do?

Your CV shows what you have done. The letter explains what it means for this job. It answers three questions:

- **Why** do you want the job?
- **How** do your experiences fit what the ad asks for?
- **Who** are you to work with?

The letter should not repeat your CV. It should point out what is most important and connect it to the job. If you need help with your CV first, see [CV basics](/knowledge-base/article/cv-grunder).

If you prefer a simpler version, there is [Cover letter in easy Swedish](/knowledge-base/article/latt-svenska-personligt-brev). If you want more ready-made templates, there is [Cover letter: templates and examples](/knowledge-base/article/personligt-brev-mall).

## How do you write a cover letter? How to build it

### 1. Greeting
If you know who is hiring, you can write "Hej Maria" (Hi Maria). If you do not know, "Hej" or "Hej och tack för annonsen" (Hi, and thank you for the ad) is enough. Avoid stiff phrases like "Till den det berör" (To whom it may concern).

### 2. Opening: create interest
The first sentences decide if the reader wants to keep reading. Skip "I am applying for the job as...". They already know that. Write why you are applying instead.

**Weaker opening:**
"I am writing to apply for the job as a salesperson that you advertised."

**Better openings:**

- *A link to the company:* "When I read about how you work with sustainable products, I understood that this is a workplace where I want to be part of the team."
- *What drives you:* "I have always liked bringing order to messy things. That is why your ad about warehouse planning caught my interest."
- *Something you have done:* "During my years in customer service, I learned that calm and clarity do more than fast answers. I want to bring that to you."
- *Someone you know:* "My former colleague told me about the atmosphere at your workplace, and it made me curious."

### 3. Why this job and this company?
Show that you have read the ad and looked at the business. Think about:

- What in the ad made you interested?
- What do you find interesting about the company?
- How does the role fit where you want to go?

**Example:** "What attracts me to the role is that it is close to the customers. I do best when I can help someone directly and see that it makes a difference."

### 4. Why you?
This is the middle part of the letter. Choose two or three things that the ad asks for and show how you have done them before. A simple structure for each one:

1. What the ad asks for
2. What you have done that is similar
3. What it led to

**Example:** "You are looking for someone who can keep track of several tasks at the same time. In my last job I was responsible for both bookings and reception, and I learned to list the most urgent things first. That meant guests and colleagues knew what to expect."

**Example:** "You want someone with good communication. I have received customers for several years and learned to explain things simply, even when the question is complicated."

### 5. Ending
Tie it together and say that you would be glad to tell more. Do not make a big promise.

**Example:** "I think my experience of meeting customers would fit well with you. I would be glad to tell you more at an interview."

### 6. Closing
"Vänliga hälsningar" (Kind regards) and your name always works. "Med vänliga hälsningar" (With kind regards) is a little more formal.

## Three complete cover letters to start from

### Letter 1: for you with experience

> Hej Sofia,
>
> When I saw your ad for a customer service employee, I thought right away that this could be the job for me. I have followed your service for some time and I like that you combine technology with a personal approach.
>
> What attracts me most to the role is the chance to help people with things that feel complicated. At my current workplace I answer customer cases by phone and chat every day. I have learned to listen to the end before I answer, and to ask for a minute when I need to check something. I also help colleagues with computer questions, which I enjoy.
>
> I am now looking for a workplace where I can keep developing in customer contact. I would be glad to tell you more at an interview.
>
> Vänliga hälsningar,
> Anna Karlsson

### Letter 2: when you change field

> Hej,
>
> After many years in restaurant kitchens I want to work in administration, and your job as receptionist caught my interest.
>
> You may wonder how a cook fits as a receptionist. In the kitchen I have learned to keep order when many things happen at once, to take orders and to stay calm under heavy pressure. Reception work has similar demands: to meet people in a friendly way, handle the phone and bookings, and know what is most important right now.
>
> I recently took a course in office programs. I have not worked in a reception before, but I learn fast and am glad to take instructions.
>
> Vänliga hälsningar,
> Erik Johansson

### Letter 3: when you apply for your first job

> Hej,
>
> I have just finished upper secondary school (gymnasiet) and I am applying for your job as shop assistant.
>
> I have not had a paid job yet, but I have been responsible for the school kiosk during breaks. I received customers, took payment and filled up goods. I like talking to people, and I am used to being on time.
>
> Your shop is close to where I live and I have shopped with you since I was small. I would like to learn more about how you work.
>
> Please get in touch.
> Lisa Andersson

## Cover letter for sales, shop and customer service

If you apply for a job in sales, in a shop or in customer service: tell how you meet people. Write what you do when a customer is unsure or unhappy, and what you like about the work. Keep it concrete and short.

## How long a cover letter should be

Write briefly, so that it fits on one page. There is no fixed word limit, but a letter that can be read in one go is easier to take in. If you write a short cover letter, keep the three parts: why the job, why you, and an ending.

## Do this

- **Be personal but factual.** Write the way you speak, a little more organized.
- **Give examples.** "I am driven" says little. A situation where you were driven says more.
- **Adapt every letter.** Read the ad and use its words. A letter that could be sent to any company is easy to spot.
- **Start strong.** The first sentence should be worth reading.
- **Show, do not tell.** Instead of "I am a good problem solver", tell about a problem you solved.

## Avoid this

- **Repeating your CV.** The letter should go deeper, not copy.
- **Speaking badly about a former employer.** Even if you have reasons, it gives a bad impression.
- **The wrong tone.** "Undertecknad anhåller härmed" (a very stiff, old-fashioned phrase) is too stiff. "Tjena" (a very casual hello) is too relaxed.
- **Spelling mistakes.** Read it through several times, and ask someone else to read it too.
- **Exaggerating.** Everything you write can come up at the interview.
- **Writing only what you want.** Also show what you can give.

## Common questions

### Do I have to write a cover letter?
It depends on the ad. If it says that a letter should be attached, write one. [Arbetsförmedlingen](https://arbetsformedlingen.se) (the Swedish Public Employment Service) also has general advice about applications.

### What should I write at the start of a cover letter?
Write why you are applying and what attracts you. Do not use the name of the job as your first sentence.

### How do I end a cover letter?
Say that you would be glad to tell more at an interview, and end with "Vänliga hälsningar" and your name.

### Can I reuse a letter?
You can reuse the structure and good phrases, but rewrite the content so that it fits each ad.

## What do I do now

Choose an ad. Write three lines: why you are applying, two things you have done that are similar to what the ad asks for, and an ending. Then build them out into a letter. If your [CV](/cv) is ready, it is easy to take examples from it.
