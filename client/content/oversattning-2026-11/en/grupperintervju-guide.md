TITLE: Group interview: what it is and how to do well
SUMMARY: What is a group interview? How it works, which questions and exercises may come up, and how to be seen without taking over.

A group interview is an interview where several candidates meet the employer at the same time. Here we go through what a group interview is, which questions and exercises may come up, and how to show your best side without taking over.

## What is a group interview?

Several applicants are interviewed at the same time, often by one or two people from the company. The aim is to see how you work together with others, and so this form is sometimes used for jobs where you meet customers or work in a team.

The set-up varies. You may meet:

- **A shared round of conversation.** Everyone answers the same questions, one at a time or in free order.
- **A group exercise.** You solve a task together while the employer watches.
- **A case.** You get a problem to discuss and present a solution.
- **Role play.** You act out a work situation, for example a meeting with a customer.

Sometimes they are combined with a short one-to-one interview.

## What does the employer look at?

The employer wants to see how you behave in a group, not only what you say:

- Do you listen to others and build on their ideas?
- Can you take space without running over anyone?
- Do you express yourself clearly?
- How do you make sure the group moves forward when things become unclear?

There is no single right way to be. A calm person who sums up can contribute as much as the one who talks the most.

## Questions that may come up in a group interview

The questions are like those in a normal interview. They are just asked in front of more people:

- Tell us briefly about yourself.
- Why did you apply for this job?
- What are you good at, and what do you want to get better at?
- What role do you usually take in a group?
- What do you do when you disagree?

You can find thoughts on several of them in [Interview questions](/knowledge-base/article/intervju-fragor) and [Tell me about yourself](/knowledge-base/article/beratta-om-dig-sjalv). Answer briefly. You share the time with others.

## How to behave in the group

### Listen first
Hear what the task is about before you suggest anything. Ask if something is unclear, it helps the whole group.

### Build on what others said
"What Sara said about time sounds wise to me, and I think we can also..." This shows that you listened.

### Make sure everyone gets space
If someone has not said anything, you can ask: "What do you think about this?" Do it in a friendly way and without singling anyone out.

### Help the group move forward
Sum up: "If I understand right, we have two suggestions. Shall we choose one?" If you are not getting anywhere, suggest an order.

### Keep track of the time
If you are slow, you can remind the group that time is limited. You do not need to lead, only keep it together.

## If you are shy or have little energy that day

Group interviews can feel big, especially if you have less energy that day.

- Prepare one or two contributions in advance, so that you have something to say early.
- You do not need to talk the most. One well-thought contribution means more than many short ones.
- Sum up or ask a question. It does not need a new idea.
- Ask in advance how the interview is set up and if you can get an adjustment, for example more time or a calmer place.

## What can go wrong

- **Taking over.** If you talk all the time, others get no space.
- **Disappearing.** If you say nothing, the employer knows nothing about you.
- **Interrupting.** Let others finish speaking.
- **Speaking badly about others' suggestions.** Rather say what you think could be added.
- **Forgetting the task.** It is about solving it, not about being seen.

## How should I think about the other candidates?

They are not your opponents during the exercise. The employer can choose several or none. What you can influence is how you cooperate, and that is also what is seen.

## Common questions

### What is a group interview?
An interview where several candidates take part at the same time, so that the employer can see how you cooperate. Read more above.

### How does a group interview work?
Most often the employer introduces themselves, you introduce yourselves, and then come questions or an exercise. Sometimes it is followed by a one-to-one interview. Feel free to ask in advance how it is set up.

### What should I wear?
The same as for a normal interview. Read [Clothes for an interview](/knowledge-base/article/kladsel-pa-intervju).

### How much should I talk?
About as much as the others. Quality weighs more than quantity.

### What do I do if someone takes over?
Wait for a gap, build on what was said and bring the conversation back. It can show calm.

## After the interview

Say thank you briefly and kindly. You can write a short thank-you afterwards, see [Thank-you note after an interview](/knowledge-base/article/tackbrev-intervju). Write down what you thought went well, because it is useful for next time. If a new round comes, see [The second interview](/knowledge-base/article/andra-intervjun).

## What do I do now

Choose one example of what you can say to build on someone else and one to bring a quiet person into the conversation. Say them out loud. More support before the day is at [Arbetsförmedlingen](https://arbetsformedlingen.se) (the Swedish Public Employment Service).
