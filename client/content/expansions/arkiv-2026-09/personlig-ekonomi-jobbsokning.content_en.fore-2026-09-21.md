Keeping track of your finances reduces stress and lets you focus on your job search. Here is what you need to know.

## Your financial rights

### A-kassa (Swedish unemployment insurance fund)
If you are a member of an a-kassa and meet the conditions:
- Benefits based on your previous income
- Up to 80% of your salary, up to a cap
- Requires that you actively look for work

**Conditions:**
- Membership for at least 12 months
- Worked at least 6 of the last 12 months
- Registered with Arbetsförmedlingen

### Basic benefit (grundersättning)
If you do not meet the conditions for income-based benefits:
- A lower benefit (about SEK 510/day)
- The same activity requirements

### Activity support (aktivitetsstöd)
If you take part in a program through Arbetsförmedlingen:
- Payments during training
- A paid internship
- The job guarantee program (jobbgarantin)

### Income support (försörjningsstöd / social assistance)
If you have no other income:
- Apply through your municipality's social services
- Based on need
- Temporary support

## Budgeting while unemployed

### Step 1: Map out your expenses
**Fixed costs (must be paid):**
- Rent/housing
- Electricity and water
- Insurance
- Phone/internet
- Any loans

**Variable costs (can be adjusted):**
- Food
- Transport
- Clothes
- Entertainment

### Step 2: Compare with your income
- How much do you get in benefits?
- Is it enough to cover your fixed costs?
- What is left over?

### Step 3: Make a realistic budget
**Prioritize:**
1. A roof over your head (rent, electricity)
2. Food
3. Health
4. Transport for job searching

### Tips for saving money

**Food:**
- Plan weekly menus
- Shop with a list
- Buy basic ingredients and cook from scratch
- Check discounted items close to their expiry date

**Transport:**
- Walk or cycle when you can
- Check if you qualify for a discounted travel card
- Plan your trips to interviews efficiently

**Subscriptions:**
- Go through all your subscriptions
- Cancel what you do not use
- Negotiate your phone and internet prices

## If you have debts

### Deal with it right away
Ignoring debt makes it worse. Contact:
- Your lender, to discuss a payment plan
- A budget and debt counselor (free through your municipality)
- Kronofogden (the Swedish Enforcement Authority) if you already have debts registered there

### Prioritizing your debts
1. Rent (risk of eviction)
2. Electricity (risk of disconnection)
3. Debts registered with Kronofogden
4. Other debts

### Debt relief (skuldsanering)
If you have large debts and cannot pay:
- Apply through Kronofogden
- Requires that you have tried other solutions first
- A chance for a fresh start

## Support and benefits worth knowing about

### Housing allowance (bostadsbidrag)
- For people with a low income
- Apply through Försäkringskassan
- Depends on your rent, income, and family situation

### Student aid (studiemedel)
If you are studying to improve your chances of getting hired:
- CSN (Swedish student aid) for approved courses
- Transition study support (omställningsstudiestöd) for people already in the workforce

### Dental care support
- Special dental care allowance for people with low income
- Frisktandvård (a fixed-fee dental care plan)

### Glasses allowance
- Available in some regions for people with low income

## Practical tips

### Keep track of payments
- Note when the money arrives
- Leave a margin for delays
- Plan your spending around the payment date

### Document everything
- Keep decisions and letters
- Note the date and the name of the person you spoke to
- Keep track of your cases

### Ask for help early
Do not wait until things become urgent. Contact:
- A budget and debt counselor
- Your employment consultant
- Social services

## Remember

Financial stress is real and heavy. But help is available. Take one step at a time, and do not be afraid to ask for support.
