From autumn 2026, an aktivitetskrav (activity requirement) applies to you if you have received försörjningsstöd (financial support from the kommun, the municipality) for some time: the kommun must offer you activities during the day, and you must take part in them to keep the support. Here is what the requirement means, what counts as an activity, what happens if you cannot come, and what rights you have.

## The requirement in short

The Riksdag (the Swedish parliament) has changed socialtjänstlagen (the Social Services Act) so that a person who has received försörjningsstöd for a continuous period must take part in activities that the kommun arranges. The idea is that your days should be filled with things that bring you closer to a job or an education, instead of the support being paid out with nothing to do during the day.

It is the kommun's socialtjänst (social services) that decides if the requirement applies to you, plans the activities together with you, and follows up that you take part. The number of hours is adapted to what you can manage, and there is a limit to how much the kommun may require. If you have small children at home, the time must be shorter. You can find the exact limits and exceptions at [Socialstyrelsen](https://www.socialstyrelsen.se) (the National Board of Health and Welfare) and on your kommun's website.

## Who the requirement does not apply to

The requirement does not apply to everyone who has försörjningsstöd. You are exempt if you, for example

- work full time, take part in a full-time programme at Arbetsförmedlingen (the Swedish Public Employment Service), or study sfi (Swedish for immigrants) full time
- receive sjukpenning, sjukersättning or aktivitetsersättning (sickness or activity benefits), or are ill in a way that corresponds to that
- are on parental leave with a small child
- are young and still go to school
- have reached retirement age

If you are unsure whether one of the exceptions applies to you, ask your handläggare (case officer) directly. It is the socialnämnd (social welfare committee) that decides this, and you have the right to know how they reasoned.

## What counts as an activity

The law describes four kinds of activities. The kommun chooses among them based on what you need.

| Kind of activity | Examples |
|---|---|
| Things that strengthen your motivation and ability to take a job or start studying | Courses, group meetings, health-promoting activities, conversations |
| Things that improve your Swedish | Language training, language cafés, studies alongside sfi |
| Things that make it easier to look for a job | Job-seeking workshops, help with your CV and applications, interview training |
| Activity at a workplace | Praktik (work placement) or arbetsträning (work training) at the kommun, an association, the region or a company |

Looking for jobs on your own at home does not count as an activity in the meaning of the law, but the plan must give you time for it. Looking for a job is still part of your week, alongside the activities.

## Your individual plan

Before the activities start, the kommun must make a plan together with you. The plan describes which activities you will take part in, how much time they take, and how much time is set aside for looking for jobs. It must be based on your situation: health, children, language, earlier experience.

Ask to get the plan in writing, and read it before you sign. If something is not right, for example if the number of hours is more than you can manage or a time clashes with picking up your child from förskola (preschool), say so right away. It is easier to change a plan before it starts than afterwards.

## If you cannot come

This is the part that worries most people, and it is worth understanding properly.

If you miss an activity without an acceptable reason, the kommun can reduce or refuse continued försörjningsstöd. The decision is made by the socialnämnd, not by the person who leads the activity, and it must be based on an assessment of your own situation.

If you are ill, you need to be able to show it. The kommun can ask for a läkarintyg (doctor's certificate) from the first day you are away because of illness. Call your vårdcentral (health centre) the same day you become ill, and tell the kommun right away that you will not come. Getting in touch in time is the single most important thing you can do.

Other reasons that normally count as acceptable are things you cannot control: a sick child with no one else to look after them, a meeting with another authority, a funeral. Always say why you could not come, preferably in advance.

## If you get a decision you do not agree with

A decision to reduce or refuse försörjningsstöd can be appealed. The decision must tell you how to do it and by when. You can ask the kommun for help writing the appeal, and you can turn to the kommun's budget- och skuldrådgivning (budget and debt counselling) or a voluntary organisation if you need support in the meantime.

Read more about how an appeal works in [What do I do if I want to appeal a decision?](/knowledge-base/article/lattsvenska-overklaga).

## What the kommun must do

The requirement also places demands on the kommun. It must

- offer activities that fill the time the plan says
- adapt the activities to what you can manage
- follow up that you take part, and document it
- report to the supervisory authority how the requirement is applied
- inform [Arbetsförmedlingen](https://arbetsformedlingen.se) that you have been assigned an activity

If you are not offered any activities even though the requirement applies to you, that is the kommun's failure, not yours. Then ask for a meeting and a written answer about when the activities will start.

## What do I do now

If you have received a letter or a phone call about the aktivitetskrav, start by asking three things: does the requirement apply to me, what does the plan mean in hours per week, and how do I report absence. Write down the answers. Then use the time in the plan that is set aside for job seeking to keep your CV up to date and apply for the jobs that suit you, so that the activities and the job seeking pull in the same direction.

## Common questions

### Can I choose the activity myself?
You can always say what you would like, and the plan must be made together with you. But it is the kommun that decides which activities are offered and which one suits you best, based on their assessment.

### Do I have to take part if I already look for jobs every day?
Yes, if the requirement applies to you. Looking for jobs on your own does not count as an activity in the meaning of the law, but the plan must give you time for it alongside the activities.

### What happens if I get a job in the meantime?
If you get a full-time job, the requirement no longer applies. If you get a part-time job, the plan must be redone so that there are fewer activities. Tell the kommun as soon as you know.

### May the kommun share information about me with Arbetsförmedlingen?
Yes. The kommun must inform Arbetsförmedlingen that you have been assigned an activity, and Arbetsförmedlingen must tell the kommun if you are already in a programme. This exchange does not require your consent, but you have the right to know what information has been shared.
