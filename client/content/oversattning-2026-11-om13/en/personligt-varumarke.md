TITLE: Personal brand and personal profile: how to build them
SUMMARY: How to shape your personal brand and write a personal profile for your CV. Simple steps, no perfection, no pretending to be someone else.

A personal brand is the picture others get of you as a professional, with or without your help. You already have one. The question is if you want to shape it yourself. Here is a simple way to do it without becoming someone else.

## What is a personal brand?

- The opinion others have of you professionally
- The combination of your skills, your personality and your values
- How you present yourself online and offline
- What you are known for in your network and your industry

It is not about becoming famous. It is about being clear about who you are and what you can give, so that the person who reads your application or your profile understands it quickly.

## Start with yourself

Before you write anything, you need to know what you want to say.

- What is important to you in working life?
- What are you good at, and what do you like to do?
- What do you want to be hired for?

You can find help in [Find your values](/knowledge-base/article/hitta-dina-varderingar), [What are your strengths?](/knowledge-base/article/upptack-dina-styrkor) and in Jobin's [personal brand](/personal-brand) tool.

Fill in the sentence: "I help [who] with [what] by [how I do it]." It does not have to be published. It is your compass.

## Personal profile: how to write it

If you search for "personal profile", you probably mean the short text at the top of a CV or on LinkedIn. It should answer three things: who you are professionally, what you can do in concrete terms and what you are looking for.

Example of the form. Replace the content with your own:

"Warehouse worker with a forklift licence and experience of picking and receiving goods. Careful and used to working in a team. Looking for work in warehouse and logistics."

Keep it short, true and without phrases that everyone uses. Do not write anything you cannot stand behind at an interview. Here is how to build the CV itself: [How to write a CV that gets results](/knowledge-base/article/cv-grunder).

## Build your brand step by step

### 1. Be clear about what you offer

A clear focus is easier to be remembered for than a wide one. "Cleaning and property service in apartment buildings" says more than "works with everything". You can widen it later.

### 2. Use the same picture everywhere

The same name, the same core message and about the same profile photo in your CV, cover letter and on the platforms you use. Change the details but keep the content. About the photo on a CV, see [Photo on your CV or not?](/knowledge-base/article/bild-pa-cv-eller-inte).

### 3. Choose platforms by profession

You do not need to be everywhere. Choose the places where people in your industry really are.

- **LinkedIn** fits many jobs. Here is how: [LinkedIn profile: from basic to outstanding](/knowledge-base/article/linkedin-profil-optimering) and the [LinkedIn optimiser](/linkedin-optimizer).
- **Your own portfolio** fits jobs where you can show work samples, see [How to build a professional portfolio](/knowledge-base/article/portfolio-guide).
- **Industry-specific sites** can fit, for example for developers or designers.

### 4. Show what you can do, without boasting

You do not have to publish anything to have a brand. If you want to be seen, you can start by commenting on other people's posts, sharing an article with a sentence of your own about it, or writing about something you have learned. Think long term: a steady, moderate pace is possible to keep up over time.

### 5. Build relationships, online and in real life

Answer in a personal way, be helpful and meet people when you can. Read more in [Networking: your hidden job market](/knowledge-base/article/natverka-for-jobb).

## Being authentic

Do not exaggerate and do not make things up. A brand that does not match you is hard to keep up, and it is hard to stand behind at an interview. You can show personality and still set limits for what you share. Remember that what you post can stay online and be copied.

## Manage your reputation

- Google your name and see what comes up. Here is how: [Google yourself](/knowledge-base/article/googla-dig-sjalv).
- Update profiles that you have left to get old, or remove them.
- Answer criticism in a calm, factual way, and take difficult discussions in private.
- Clean up and improve your digital presence: [Clean up and improve your digital presence](/knowledge-base/article/digital-narvaro-rensning).

## Common mistakes

- **Copying someone else.** Take inspiration, but write in your own words.
- **Trying to be perfect.** It is enough to be clear and true.
- **Spreading different messages.** Different facts in different places create doubt.
- **Believing that it is fast.** It takes time to be known for something, and it can feel slow.

## What do I do now

Write your sentence ("I help ...") and your short profile text. Google yourself. Then update your CV and, if you have one, your LinkedIn profile so that they say the same thing.

## Common questions

### What is a personal brand?

The picture others have of you as a professional. You have one even if you have never worked on it.

### Do I need to be active on social media?

No. It can help in some jobs, but a clear profile, a good CV and a network you keep in touch with go a long way. About other platforms than LinkedIn, see [Social media in your job search](/knowledge-base/article/sociala-medier-jobbsokning).

### How do you write a personal profile for a CV?

Short, concrete and true: who you are, what you can do and what you are looking for. See the section above.
