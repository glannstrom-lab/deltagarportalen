TITLE: Personality types and career choice: what tests say
SUMMARY: What Big Five, MBTI and Holland codes can give you for your career choice, and what they cannot answer. Plus a simple way to find what fits you.

Your personality can give clues about what feels easy and what takes energy in a job. It cannot tell you which job to have. Here is what some common models describe, how you can use them as a basis for thinking, and what they cannot answer.

## First: what personality tests can and cannot do

- They can give you words for things you already recognise in yourself.
- They are good to have with you when you think about work environment, ways of working and tasks.
- They cannot predict if you will be successful, be happy or get a certain job, and I cannot show that any type fits one job better than another.
- You are more than a code of letters. The same person can be happy in different jobs, and culture, manager and colleagues also matter.

Use the result as a question to yourself, not as an answer. If you want to try one later: [Free job test: what a test can and cannot say](/knowledge-base/article/yrkestest-sa-anvander-du-det).

## Some common models

### Big Five

It describes five traits that every person has to different degrees, not as types: openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness and emotional stability. Each trait is a scale. Being high or low is not an advantage or a weakness in itself.

### Myers-Briggs (MBTI)

It divides people into sixteen types based on four pairs of preferences: where you get energy, how you take in information, how you make decisions and how you organise everyday life. See it as something to talk about, not as a judgement about what you are right for.

### Holland codes (RIASEC)

This is the model made to connect interests with work environments. Six interest areas:

| Letter | Interest | Is about |
|---|---|---|
| R | Realistic | Practical work with tools, machines or nature |
| I | Investigative | Analysing, investigating, solving problems |
| A | Artistic | Creating, expressing yourself, finding your own solutions |
| S | Social | Helping, teaching, supporting |
| E | Enterprising | Leading, convincing, driving things forward |
| C | Conventional | Order, systems, accuracy |

You describe yourself with a combination of a few letters. It is a description of interests, not a prediction of success.

## Personal qualities: examples

Are you looking for words for your qualities? Some examples: careful, responsible, flexible, calm under pressure, social, independent, curious, solution-focused. Choose the ones that are true and that you can support with a concrete example. You can find more in [Personal qualities: examples for CV and interview](/knowledge-base/article/personliga-egenskaper-exempel).

### A detail-oriented person

To be detail-oriented means that you notice small differences and mistakes and like things to be right. It can be a strength where accuracy is needed. It can also take time and energy if the work needs fast decisions. What fits depends on the task.

## Jobs without much contact with people

The question "jobs without contact with people" may be about how much contact you have energy for. Many jobs mean contact with colleagues, a manager or customers, and the difference is in how much, how and when. Ask in the ad or at the interview:

- How much of the day am I alone, and how much in contact with others?
- Is the contact planned or spontaneous?
- Can I get clear instructions in writing?

You do not have to say why you ask. If you need adjustments, you can read about support in [Adjustments at the workplace](/knowledge-base/article/anpassningar-arbetsplatsen).

## How to find out what fits you

### Self-reflection

- When do you feel most energetic at work?
- Which tasks do you put off?
- Do you prefer structure or freedom?
- What did you like to do as a child or young person?

### Energy log

For one week: write down what gives energy and what takes it. After a week you can see patterns.

### Ask others

Ask a friend, a former colleague or a handledare (support person) how they would describe you. Read old references.

### Use tools

Jobin's [interest guide](/interest-guide) can give ideas about jobs to explore. See it as inspiration, not as an answer sheet.

## Career choice: a simple way

1. Write down which tasks, places and ways of working give you energy.
2. Write down what takes energy and what you want to avoid.
3. Explore some jobs that contain what you want. [What should I work with?](/knowledge-base/article/vad-ska-jag-jobba-med) helps you go on.
4. Talk to someone who does the job. An [information interview](/knowledge-base/article/informationsintervju-guide) is one way.
5. Try, if you can: praktik (work placement), a temporary job or following someone for a day.

Also read [Find your dream job](/knowledge-base/article/hitta-ditt-drommjobb) and [Dream job analysis](/knowledge-base/article/dromjobbsanalys).

## Pitfalls

- **Taking the result too literally.** It is a tool, not a fate.
- **Choosing by trend.** Choose by what you really want and can develop.
- **Forgetting the environment.** Manager, colleagues and culture also matter.
- **Believing that you cannot change.** You can learn new things and grow into tasks.

## Development opportunities

The question "development opportunities" is about what you can develop in yourself and in a role. Start from what you have and what you lack, and see [Plan your skills development](/knowledge-base/article/kompetensutveckling-plan).

## What do I do now

Do the energy log for one week. Then choose one or two jobs that seem to give you more of what gives energy, and read about them or talk to someone who works there.

## Common questions

### Which jobs fit my personality type?

You cannot read that from a test. The test can give ideas about what you like, but your choice of job should be based on what you want, can do and have the chance to do.

### Are personality tests reliable?

Different models have different support, and none can predict how a job will be for you. Use them for reflection. Some employers use tests in recruitment, see [Work psychology tests](/knowledge-base/article/arbetspsykologiska-tester).

### Is it wrong to be introverted in a social job?

No. A social job can cost more energy if you are introverted, and then you can plan for rest. It is a question of balance, not of whether you are allowed.
