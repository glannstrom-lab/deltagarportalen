TITLE: CV for ATS systems: how to format it right
SUMMARY: Practical format tips when you apply through an ATS: layout, headings, file type and a test you can do yourself in Notepad.

If you apply for a job through a form where you upload your CV, an ATS (a recruitment system) often receives it. This page is a practical format guide: how to build your CV so that the text comes through and ends up in the right place when the system reads it. If you want to know what an ATS is and how employers use it, read [ATS systems and your CV: how they work and how to adapt](/knowledge-base/article/ats-system-guide).

Are you looking for an ATS to use as an employer? This page is written for people who apply for jobs.

## What an ATS does with your CV, in short

ATS means Applicant Tracking System, a system that keeps track of job applicants. The system collects applications and tries to read details from the file you upload, such as name, job titles, years and skills. What the system does next, and whether anything is removed automatically, is decided by the employer through the settings. You cannot know this in advance. What you can control is that the text in your CV can be read.

Systems that are used include Teamtailor, Workday, Greenhouse and Lever.

## The most important test: copy to Notepad

Before you change anything, do this:

1. Open your CV file and select all the text.
2. Copy it and paste it into Notepad (Anteckningar in Swedish) or another empty text document.
3. Read it. Is everything there? Does it come in the right order? Do dates and job titles stay together?

If the text is messy or parts are missing, an ATS may also have trouble reading it. Then you should make the layout simpler.

## Format tips

### Layout

- Use one column. Several columns, text boxes and tables can be read in the wrong order.
- Put your contact details in the main page, not in the header or footer.
- Keep text as text. Icons and images with text in them cannot be copied.

### Fonts and headings

- Use common fonts and clear headings.
- Use headings that everyone knows: profile, work experience, education, skills, languages. A heading like "My journey" tells neither the system nor the reader anything.

### File type

- Upload the file type that the advert or the form asks for. If it does not say, PDF or Word are common choices.
- Use a PDF that you saved from a text document, not a scanned picture of a paper. With a picture you cannot select the text.
- Do not protect the file with a password.

### Dates and job titles

- Write dates in the same way all the way through, for example month and year.
- Write the job title, employer and period clearly for each job.

### Words from the advert

Read the advert and note which words it uses for tasks and requirements. If you have the experience, write it with the same words. If the advert says "kundservice" (customer service) and your CV says "kundbemötande" (customer care), a word search may miss you, even though you did the job. Only write what you have really done. You can write both the short form and the full name the first time, for example "CRM (customer register)".

## A simple structure to start from

```
FIRST NAME LAST NAME
Phone | Email | Place

PROFILE
Two or three sentences about who you are as a worker.

WORK EXPERIENCE
Job title | Employer | Place
Month year – month year
- What you did
- What you achieved

EDUCATION
Course | School | Year

SKILLS
Tools, systems and driving licence

LANGUAGES
Language and level
```

The CV builder in Jobin has templates, and you can change the template without writing the content again. You can test the finished file with the Notepad test above.

## What not to do

- Do not hide keywords in white text. A person who selects the page will see them, and it is your real experience that you want someone to read.
- Do not copy the advert straight into your CV.
- Do not write skills that you do not have.

## The application form is also part of your application

The system can sort by the answers you give in the form, for example questions about driving licence, languages or availability, if the employer has set it up that way. Read the questions carefully and answer truthfully.

## Do you need a graphic CV too?

A more designed CV can be good when you give it directly to a person. The same content can be in both versions, so you do not need to write it twice.

## What to do now

1. Do the Notepad test on your current CV.
2. Make the layout simpler if something comes out wrong.
3. Read the advert you are applying for and compare its words with your CV.
4. If you need a base to start from: [CV templates and examples](/knowledge-base/article/cv-mallar-exempel) and [How to write a CV that gets results](/knowledge-base/article/cv-grunder). You can find more tips in [ATS optimisation](/knowledge-base/article/ats-optimering).
