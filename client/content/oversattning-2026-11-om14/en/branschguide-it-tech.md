TITLE: The IT industry: what jobs exist and how to get in
SUMMARY: What the IT industry is, what kinds of jobs exist, ways in with and without education, and where to find pay statistics for each job.

The IT industry is a group of workplaces and jobs that build, run, protect and explain digital systems. This means everything from apps and websites to servers, networks and data. This guide gives you an overview of the kinds of jobs that exist, what they mean in daily work and the ways in, with and without education. You can find pay in official statistics. Further down you can read how to look it up.

## What is the IT industry, and what is tech?

IT and tech mean almost the same thing. IT is the older word. It is usually about systems and operations in organisations. Tech is used more often about companies that build digital products. The jobs are at IT companies, but also at almost all other employers. Municipalities, regions, banks, shop chains and industrial companies have their own IT departments.

This means you can work with IT without working at an IT company. It is also why a job such as "IT manager" can look very different from one place to another.

## What kinds of jobs are there in IT?

You can sort the jobs by what people do. Job titles differ between employers, so always read the tasks in the job ad.

| Focus | What you do | Example jobs |
|---|---|---|
| Build | Write program code, build services and apps | Frontend, backend and full-stack developers, programmers |
| Design | Make interfaces easy to understand and test them with users | UX and UI designer, interaction designer |
| Lead | Decide what to build and keep the work together | Product owner, project manager, release manager |
| Run | Make sure systems and servers work | System administrator, DevOps engineer, IT support |
| Protect | Find weak points and handle incidents | IT security specialist |
| Understand data | Analyse data and build models from it | Data analyst, data scientist |
| Explain | Write manuals and instructions for technical products | Technical writer (teknikinformatör) |

### What does a programmer do?

A programmer writes, reads and corrects code. A big part of the time goes to understanding an existing system, finding and fixing errors, testing, and talking to colleagues about what to do. The programming language depends on the employer, for example Python, Java, C# or JavaScript.

### What does a technical writer do?

A technical writer makes technical products easy to understand for the people who use them, for example with manuals, help texts and instructions. It is a job for you if you like both language and technology.

### What does a product owner do?

The product owner decides what a development team works on and in which order, based on what users and the business need. The job needs more talking than coding.

### What does an IT manager do?

An IT manager is responsible for making sure an organisation's systems, equipment and IT security work. In a small company, one person may do most of it alone. In a large organisation, an IT head leads a team.

## What is the pay in IT?

We do not write pay figures here. Pay differs a lot between jobs, places, employers and experience, and figures get old quickly. Instead, look up what you need yourself, for example "python developer salary" or "product owner salary".

- **[SCB's Lönesök](https://www.scb.se/lonesok)** (SCB is Statistics Sweden; Lönesök means salary search) shows the average monthly pay before tax for a job. It is divided by, for example, level of education, age and private or public sector. Search for the name of the job. If you find no result, try a shorter word.
- The figures are averages. SCB says itself that they do not take experience or responsibility into account, and that small job groups are less certain.
- If you want to compare which type of programmer or role earns most, search for several similar jobs and compare. There is no single answer that is true for everyone.
- Before a pay negotiation, [Pay negotiation: how to get the pay you deserve](/knowledge-base/article/loneforhandling-guide) can help.

## Ways into IT

There is no single way in. Which way suits you depends on where you are now and how much time you have.

### Education

- **Yrkeshögskola** (higher vocational education) is education after upper secondary school that is close to working life. You can find the courses and rules at [Myndigheten för yrkeshögskolan](https://www.myh.se) (the Swedish Agency for Higher Vocational Education).
- **University and university college** have programmes in, for example, computer engineering, information systems and interaction design. Search on [antagning.se](https://www.antagning.se).
- **Komvux** (adult education) gives you basic eligibility or courses to build on. Read about how you can study with financial support in [Studying with financial support: which way suits you](/knowledge-base/article/studier-med-ersattning).
- **Certificates** from technology companies can add to your skills, but their value depends on the employer. Check what the ads you want to apply for really ask for.

### Without formal education

- **Build something you can show.** A few projects of your own, preferably with open code, say more than a list of courses. How to do this is in [Build a portfolio](/knowledge-base/article/portfolio-guide).
- **Start in a nearby job.** IT support, testing and documentation can be a way into a team.
- **Work placement.** Arbetsförmedlingen (the Swedish Public Employment Service) can sometimes help with a work placement. Read [Work placement as a way in](/knowledge-base/article/praktik-som-vag-in).
- **Validation.** If you already know a lot, you can get it certified. See [Validation of vocational skills](/knowledge-base/article/validering-av-yrkeskunskaper).

## What do employers ask for right now?

The fastest way to know is to read the ads. Search for the job in [Platsbanken](https://arbetsformedlingen.se) (the job board of Arbetsförmedlingen) and note which languages, tools and ways of working come up again and again. Arbetsförmedlingen's yrkesbarometer (occupational barometer) also judges how the situation is for a job today and in five years. How to read it is in [Jobs of the future](/knowledge-base/article/framtidens-jobb).

Besides the technology, ads often ask for the ability to work together and to explain. You can show this with examples from earlier jobs and daily life, see [Skills inventory](/knowledge-base/article/kompetensinventering-guide).

## What do I do now

1. Choose a focus from the table that you are curious about.
2. Search for the job in SCB's Lönesök and in Platsbanken. Then you see both the pay level and what employers ask for.
3. Make a list of what you already know that fits, and what is missing. [The skills gap analysis](/skills-gap-analysis) helps you with that.
4. Choose a way in: a course, a project, a work placement or a first application.

## Common questions

### Can I work in IT without education?

Yes, it is possible, but it depends on the job and the employer. Some jobs need a degree. Others look more at what you can show. Read the ad and see what it really asks for.

### Which IT jobs have the best pay?

You find out best by comparing the jobs in SCB's Lönesök. Remember that the average mixes beginners and experienced people.

### Is IT a good field to change to?

It depends on which job you aim for and how the situation is for that job. Look at Arbetsförmedlingen's yrkesbarometer for the job, and talk to someone who already works in it.
