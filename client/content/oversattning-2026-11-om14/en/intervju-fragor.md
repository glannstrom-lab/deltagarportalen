TITLE: Common interview questions and how to answer them
SUMMARY: The most common interview questions, from "tell me about yourself" to situation questions, with ideas for how to answer each one.

Here are the interview questions that are most common to prepare for, with ideas for how to answer each one. The goal is not to learn answers by heart. People can hear that. The goal is to think through what you want to say, so that you can say it in your own words even when you are nervous.

This page is an overview. If you want to go deeper on one question, we link to more help under each heading.

## How to use the list

Read the questions with your own background in mind. Write down short notes, not full answers. Then say the answers out loud, if you can to another person. [Interview practice: how to train](/knowledge-base/article/intervjutraning-sa-ovar-du) shows you how, and [Prepare for your interview](/knowledge-base/article/intervju-forberedelser) covers the whole preparation. You can also practise in the Interview simulator in Jobin.

Use the list as a template for your own preparation: one line for each question, and two or three notes under each.

## Questions about you

### Tell me briefly about yourself

The interview often starts here. Think of it as a short version of your cover letter. It is about you as a worker, not about your private life. A plan that works has three parts: where you are now, what you have done that matters for the job, and why you are applying for this job.

> "I have worked in warehouses for five years, most recently as a forklift driver at a distribution centre. I am used to keeping order in a flow of goods when things move fast. Now I am applying to you because you use a system I already know, and I want a workplace with fixed hours."

Keep it to one or two minutes. The interviewer is asking who you are as a worker, not for your whole life story.

### What are your strengths?

Choose two or three that fit the job and give one example of each. A strength without an example is only a claim. Say the strength, tell when you showed it, and connect it to the job.

More help: [Strengths and weaknesses in an interview: example answers](/knowledge-base/article/styrkor-svagheter-intervju).

### What is your weakness?

Choose something that is true but not important for the job, and tell how you handle it. Answers that are really praise ("I am too careful") sound like you are avoiding the question. An honest answer with an action is stronger.

More help: [Strengths and weaknesses in an interview](/knowledge-base/article/styrkor-svagheter-intervju).

## Questions about your background

### Why did you leave your last job?

Keep the answer short, factual and forward-looking. Do not speak badly about a former employer, even if you had reasons. An interview is not the place to get justice, and criticism of a former boss can make the listener wonder how you would talk about them.

### Why do you have a gap in your CV?

Answer directly, without excuses, and move on to what you are doing now. Illness, parental leave, care of a family member, studies and unemployment are things that happen. Tell what matters for the job: in short, what happened, and that you are ready now. You decide how much to say about your health.

> "I was on sick leave for a period and I have been well again since the spring. This autumn I took a course in accounting to get started again."

### Tell me about a time when you handled a difficult situation

This is a behaviour question. It is best answered with a story from your own working life. The usual method is called STAR: situation, task, action, result. Spend most time on the action, that is, what you did. Two sentences are enough for the background.

More help: [The STAR method: how to answer interview questions](/knowledge-base/article/kompetensbaserad-intervju-star-metoden).

## Situation questions and behaviour questions

Behaviour questions often start with "tell me about a time when ...": you handled a conflict, made a mistake, or took the first step. Situation questions instead describe an imagined situation: "What do you do if a customer is unhappy and your manager is not there?"

- Behaviour questions: choose a real event and tell it with STAR.
- Situation questions: tell how you would think, step by step, and if you can, what you did in a similar situation.

Prepare three or four stories from your working life. The same story can often answer several questions, depending on what you stress. This is easier than finding an example on the spot. If you have little work experience, examples from studies, volunteer work, a work placement or everyday life also work.

## Questions about the job and the company

### Why are you applying for this job?

Connect something you want to do with something the workplace does. Read the job advert again the evening before and find two concrete things: one task you want to do, and one thing about the company that you have really found out.

### Why should we hire you?

Say briefly what you can do that matches what the advert asks for, with one example. You do not need to beat anyone else. You only need to show that you fit the task.

### Where do you see yourself in five years?

This question is rarely about your dreams. It checks if the job suits you over time. An answer that you want to become good at this work and grow in it is fine. You do not need a career plan.

### What salary do you expect?

Have an answer ready. Check salary statistics for the job and the area before the interview and think of a range. You can read more about how to say it in [Salary expectations in your application](/knowledge-base/article/lonekrav-i-ansokan).

## Questions you can ask yourself

Have two or three ready. Some examples:

- What do the first months look like for the person who gets the job?
- What makes someone succeed in this role at your company?
- What happens next in the process, and when can I expect to hear something?

You can find more ideas in [Questions to ask the employer in an interview](/knowledge-base/article/fragor-att-stalla-till-arbetsgivaren).

## About being nervous

Being nervous is normal. Two things can help: arrive in good time so that you can settle, and bring notes. It is fine to say at the start that you have them with you. If you lose your train of thought, you can say so and ask to start again.

## What to do now

1. Choose five of the questions above and write two or three notes for each.
2. Prepare three stories from your working life or other parts of your life.
3. Say the answers out loud, best to another person or in the Interview simulator.
4. Write down three questions to ask yourself.
