Sick Pay, the Qualifying Deduction, and VAB – When You Are New to a Job
Mikael Glännström, Arbetskonsulent
Updated 8/24/2026

Who pays when you are sick or caring for a sick child, what the qualifying deduction (karensavdrag) is, when a medical certificate is required, and who to call.

Getting sick, or needing to stay home with a sick child, can feel extra uncertain when you have just started a new job. Here is who pays, in what order, and who you should actually call.

Step one: you report sick to your employer, not to an authority

If you get sick, the first step is always to tell your employer, as soon as you can, following the routines that apply at your workplace. You do not contact Försäkringskassan (the Swedish Social Insurance Agency) at this stage – they come in later, if the period of sick leave continues for a while.

Who pays when you are sick, and in what order

Responsibility shifts in two steps:

Who	What applies
Your employer	Pays sick pay during an initial period of sick leave, with a qualifying deduction at the start of the period
Försäkringskassan	Takes over and pays sickness benefit if the period of sick leave continues longer than the period your employer is responsible for

You do not need to keep track of exactly when responsibility shifts over – that is handled between your employer and Försäkringskassan. Your task is to report sick on time and hand in the certificates that are requested once the period of sick leave has gone on for a while.

If you are employed by the hour, or do not have the right to sick pay from an employer for other reasons, Försäkringskassan handles the whole calculation instead, including the qualifying deduction, from the first day.

What is the qualifying deduction, and why does it exist

The qualifying deduction (karensavdrag) is a part of your sick pay that is deducted at the start of a period of sick leave, no matter what time of day you get sick or what your schedule looks like. The idea behind it is that sick leave should carry a small cost for the employee too, not only for the employer – it is a rule set out in law, not something your employer has made up.

One thing worth knowing: if you get sick again shortly after being back at work, it is often counted as a continuation of the same period of sick leave rather than a new one, and then no new qualifying deduction is made. Exactly where the line is can depend on whether the workplace has a collective agreement – ask your employer or your union if you are unsure in a specific situation.

When is a medical certificate required

For the first days of a period of sick leave, it is normally enough to report that you are sick yourself, without a certificate. If the period of sick leave continues, a medical certificate is required, both for your employer to continue paying sick pay and later for Försäkringskassan to pay sickness benefit. Exactly when the certificate is required can differ depending on whether the workplace has a collective agreement that changes the usual limit – ask your manager or HR if you are unsure, instead of guessing.

VAB – caring for a sick child

VAB, "vård av barn" (care of a child), is the compensation you can apply for when you need to stay home to take care of a sick child instead of going to work. It is called temporary parental benefit and is paid by Försäkringskassan, not by your employer.

Two things to keep separate:

You report VAB to Försäkringskassan – that is where the application for compensation goes.
You tell your employer separately that you are absent, just as with your own illness, so they know you will not be coming in.

If the period of VAB continues for a while, Försäkringskassan can ask for a certificate to confirm that the child still needs care, in the same way as with your own illness.

Who do you call, and when

A quick rule of thumb:

You are sick, at the start of the period → tell your employer.
You are sick, and it continues for a while → Försäkringskassan takes over, often automatically based on your employer's report, but make sure you have handed in the certificates that are required.
Your child is sick → apply for VAB with Försäkringskassan, and tell your employer that you are away.
Unsure about the qualifying deduction, certificate deadlines, or what applies to your employment → ask your employer, Försäkringskassan, or your union. No one expects you to know this by heart when you are new.

What to do now

Find out what applies for reporting sick at your workplace before you need to use it – who you call, and whether there is an app or system for it. Save Försäkringskassan's contact details somewhere easy to find. And do not feel strange about asking your manager directly how the qualifying deduction or medical certificates work – it is a question everyone who is new to a job has.

Frequently asked questions
Do I need to have worked for a certain time before I get sick pay?

Sick pay is linked to being employed, not primarily to how long you have worked. There are exceptions for very short employments – if you are unsure, ask your employer or your union about your specific situation.

What happens if I get sick during my probationary period?

The same rules apply as for other employment – being on probation does not change your right to sick pay.

Can I take VAB for children other than my own?

In some cases, for example if you are the one who actually cares for the child day to day even if you are not the legal guardian. If your situation is not the usual family picture, check with Försäkringskassan what applies to you.
