Your education
Your first months
Swedish
New in Sweden

What you need to start working here — the paperwork, the authorities and the language.

If you have education or a profession from another country, there are two things to sort out: what your education corresponds to here, and whether your profession requires a Swedish licence.

Have your education assessed

The Swedish Council for Higher Education (UHR) assesses education from other countries and writes a statement of what it corresponds to in Sweden. It is free of charge, and you can attach the statement when you apply for jobs or studies. UHR assesses upper secondary education, post-secondary vocational education and higher education.

Do not have your certificates any more? Apply anyway — UHR explains on their site what can be done when documents are missing.

Does your profession require a licence?

Some professions you may not work in without a Swedish licence, however much experience you have. This includes many jobs in healthcare and teaching. In that case the professional authority assesses your education — not UHR.

Healthcare: the National Board of Health and Welfare decides on licences and protected professional titles. The route differs depending on whether you were educated inside or outside the EU/EEA, and the Swedish language requirement is high — the level is stated on their site.

Do you need a work permit?

If you are a citizen of an EU/EEA country or of a Nordic country you do not need a work permit. You may start working straight away, and you should not contact Migrationsverket — move on to the next tab.

Migrationsverket — EU/EEA citizens
Opens in a new tab

If you are a citizen of a country outside the EU/EEA a work permit is usually required, and as a rule you apply before travelling here. If you already live here with a residence permit on other grounds, different rules may apply.

We deliberately do not state any amounts or processing times here. They change every year, and an outdated figure on this page could cost you a permit. Migrationsverket always has what applies right now.

What next?

Once you know what your education corresponds to: write it in your CV with the Swedish equivalent in brackets, so a Swedish employer understands what they are reading.
